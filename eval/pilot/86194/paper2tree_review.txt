**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** contribution linking lipid homeostasis to endogenous protein aggregation via ESCRT dysfunction at lysosomes, supported by an impressive breadth of orthogonal approaches. However, the central mechanistic claim—that ESCRT disruption mediates lipid-induced aggregation—rests on **incomplete** evidence, as the causal chain from lipid accumulation to ESCRT impairment to protein aggregation is established only correlatively, key functional assays lack adequate replication (n=1 for proteasome activity), and the VPS4A rescue experiments provide only modest support for the proposed mechanism.

**Summary**

This paper uses genome-wide CRISPRi screening in K562 cells with ProteoStat dye as a readout to identify modulators of endogenous protein aggregation state. The screens recover known proteostasis regulators (proteasome, vATPase, ribosomal subunits) and uncover a previously unappreciated link between lipid homeostasis and protein aggregation. Specifically, genetic perturbations that increase lipid uptake (MYLIP knockdown) or impair lysosomal lipid catabolism (PSAP, GNPTAB knockdown) drive accumulation of sphingomyelins (SM) and cholesterol esters (CE), which correlate with increased ProteoStat staining. Using Lyso-IP proteomics with differential extraction, the authors find that 9 of the 12 most enriched detergent-insoluble lysosomal proteins in MYLIP KD cells are ESCRT pathway components. They propose a model wherein lipid dysregulation traps ESCRT-III assemblies at the lysosomal membrane, impairing membrane repair and microautophagy, leading to lysosomal membrane permeabilization and protein aggregate accumulation.

The study is ambitious in scope, integrating genome-wide genetic screens, targeted lipidomics, quantitative lysosomal proteomics, and multiple functional assays to systematically address alternative mechanisms. The identification of a lipid-ESCRT-proteostasis axis is conceptually interesting and potentially relevant to aging and lysosomal storage diseases. However, the paper's central mechanistic claim—that ESCRT disruption is the primary mediating mechanism—is not fully substantiated by the data presented.

**Strengths**

- **Genome-wide CRISPRi screen design and validation (high support):** The recovery of expected proteostasis regulators (proteasome subunits, vATPases, ribosomal proteins) with biologically interpretable directionality provides strong internal validation. The batch retest of 286 genes independently confirms screen hits, and pharmacological validation with MG132 and Bafilomycin A1 further corroborates genetic findings.

- **Identification of lipid homeostasis genes as novel aggregation modulators (high support):** Multiple coherent hits in lipid uptake (LDLR, MYLIP, SCAP, MBTPS2) and lysosomal enzyme trafficking (GNPTAB, IGF2R, LYSET, PSAP) pathways converge on lysosomal lipid metabolism, representing a genuine discovery from an unbiased approach.

- **Genetic epistasis experiments (high support):** LDLR KD is epistatic to MYLIP KD, and MYLIP/PSAP double KD dramatically exacerbates the phenotype while LDLR/PSAP double KD alleviates it. These experiments, combined with environmental manipulation (delipidated serum, LDL/cholesterol add-back), rigorously establish the directionality of the lipid-proteostasis connection.

- **Lyso-IP proteomics revealing ESCRT enrichment (high support):** The finding that 9/12 top insoluble lysosomal proteins are ESCRT components spanning multiple sub-complexes is statistically striking and highly specific. The critical control showing vATPases enriched in the total but not insoluble fraction rules out the trivial explanation that all increased lysosomal proteins become insoluble.

- **Systematic exclusion of alternative mechanisms (high support for pH):** The pHLARE sensor, validated with a standard curve, convincingly shows no lysosomal pH shift with MYLIP, PSAP, or LDLR KD. The antioxidant uncoupling experiment cleanly dissociates lipid peroxidation from protein aggregation.

- **PSAP saposin rescue system (high support):** The modular analysis demonstrating that no single saposin is sufficient or necessary for rescue elegantly supports the model that pleiotropic lipid changes, rather than a single lipid species, drive the phenotype.

- **ProteoStat-lipid cross-reactivity controls (high support):** GUV experiments and MBCD extraction convincingly rule out direct ProteoStat binding to cholesterol or cholesterol-rich membranes.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Proteasome activity data are critically underpowered (low support; n=1):** The claim that proteasome activity is "unaffected" by lipid-modifying KDs is based on a single unreplicated experiment (Figure 6H). This is scientifically insufficient to rule out proteasomal involvement, particularly given that van der Kant et al. (2019) reported CE effects on proteasome activity. This experiment must be replicated (minimum n=3) with statistical testing before the claim can stand. This is especially important because proteasome impairment is a plausible alternative mediating mechanism.

- **ESCRT "disruption" is inferred, not demonstrated (medium support for root claim):** The central thesis states that lipid dysregulation drives aggregation "through disruption of the ESCRT pathway," but detergent insolubility of ESCRT components is not equivalent to functional impairment. ESCRT-III normally forms detergent-insoluble polymers during its functional cycle. No direct measurement of ESCRT function—intraluminal vesicle formation, microautophagy flux, or membrane repair kinetics—is provided. The authors should either provide a direct functional assay of ESCRT activity or substantially temper the language throughout (including the title) from "disruption" to "association" or "accumulation."

- **VPS4A rescue is insufficient to support the mechanistic model (medium support):** VPS4A overexpression only modestly rescues MYLIP KD-induced aggregation and LMP, leaving the majority of the phenotype unexplained. Without verification of VPS4A overexpression levels/activity and without explaining what accounts for the unrescued fraction, the claim that ESCRT dysfunction mediates lipid-induced aggregation is substantially weakened. The authors should either demonstrate VPS4A expression levels and test catalytically dead VPS4A as a control, or explicitly acknowledge that ESCRT disruption may be one of several parallel mechanisms rather than the primary one.

- **Microautophagy claim lacks direct evidence (low support):** The assertion that microautophagy, rather than macroautophagy, delivers misfolded proteins to lysosomes is based entirely on indirect pattern matching (ubiquitin-low lysosomal aggregates, absence of macroautophagy gene effects, UBPY KD data). No direct measurement of microautophagy flux is provided, and chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) is not discussed as an alternative. The authors should either provide direct evidence for microautophagy involvement or present this as one of several possible pathways.

- **ProteoStat as a proxy for protein aggregation needs stronger orthogonal validation:** ProteoStat is the primary readout throughout the paper, yet its specificity for bona fide protein aggregates versus lipid-protein complexes or other lysosomal storage material is not rigorously validated in the cellular context. The Lyso-IP silver stain is supportive but semi-quantitative. Given that lipids and proteins co-accumulate in lysosomes under these conditions, the authors should identify at least some of the aggregating protein species (e.g., by mass spectrometry of the insoluble Lyso-IP fraction from multiple conditions) or provide additional aggregate-specific assays.

- **Single cell line limits generalizability:** All experiments are performed in K562 cells, a rapidly dividing chronic myelogenous leukemia line. The root claim about "endogenous protein aggregation" implicitly suggests broad relevance, including to post-mitotic cells where aggregation is most pathologically significant. Validation of key findings (ProteoStat increase with MYLIP/PSAP KD, ESCRT insolubility) in at least one additional cell type, ideally a non-cancerous or post-mitotic line, is important for the paper's conceptual impact.

- **SM/CE correlation analysis may be overfitted (medium support):** The high R² values (up to 0.968 for SM) are reported from regressions with what appears to be only 6–8 data points. With so few points, R² is inherently inflated and sensitive to individual outliers. SM and CE levels likely co-vary across perturbations, making it impossible to distinguish which lipid class (or whether total lipid load) is causal. The authors should report the exact number of data points per regression, show confidence intervals, and discuss the co-variance issue explicitly.

- **Speculative mechanistic model lacks experimental support (low support):** The proposed model that SM overabundance hinders ceramide-rich domain coalescence and prevents negative membrane curvature required for ESCRT resolution is entirely speculative. While intellectually stimulating, it is presented without any supporting experimental data from this study. This should be clearly labeled as a hypothesis requiring future testing.

**Minor Comments**

- The PSAP full-length rescue achieves only p=0.07 versus control (Figure 2—figure supplement 2D). While close to significance, describing this as "largely rescued" is generous. The authors should either increase sample size to achieve definitive significance or temper the language.

- The pharmacological chaperone inhibition time-course data (Figure 1—figure supplement 1C) is stated as n=1 for some inhibitors. These should be replicated or removed.

- The paper states these are "the first CRISPRi genome-wide screens on unmodified mammalian cells," but K562 cells stably expressing KRAB-dCas9 are modified. This claim should be rephrased.

- GSEA results are shown for "a subset" of GO terms. Full GSEA results should be provided in supplementary materials to allow assessment of specificity and rule out cherry-picking.

- The characterization of MYLIP KD effects on DQ-Red BSA protease activity as "modest and incoherent" is subjective. MYLIP KD does reduce protease activity (Figure 6E), which could contribute to aggregate accumulation even if it is not the primary mechanism. This should be discussed more transparently.

- The Lyso-IP proteomics was performed only for MYLIP KD. Profiling at least PSAP KD would substantially strengthen the generalizability of the ESCRT finding across lipid perturbation conditions.

- The quantification methods for microscopy colocalization (thresholds, algorithms) should be described in more detail in the Methods section.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper presents a conceptually interesting lipid-ESCRT-proteostasis connection supported by an impressive breadth of approaches (42/68 claims at high support), but three critical issues must be addressed: the proteasome activity data require adequate replication (n≥3), the ESCRT "disruption" claim requires either direct functional evidence or substantial tempering of language throughout the paper (including the title), and the VPS4A rescue data need to be contextualized with expression-level verification and a more honest assessment of the incomplete rescue. These revisions are essential before the mechanistic model meets eLife's standards for convincing evidence.
