**eLife Assessment**
This valuable study presents solid evidence linking ROS signaling, vimentin expression, and collagen-guided epithelial projections during zebrafish caudal fin regeneration, though several central mechanistic claims rest on incomplete evidence due to reliance on pharmacological inhibitors with known off-target effects, absence of stable genetic mutants, and difficulty distinguishing correlation from causation in the collagen-epithelial guidance model.

**Summary**
This study proposes a signaling pathway in which wound-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) activate NFκB, which drives vimentin expression in a population of cells at the wound margin. Vimentin-positive cells then regulate collagen expression and the dynamic reorganization of collagen fibers into projections that physically guide epithelial cell extensions during caudal fin regeneration in zebrafish larvae. The authors generate a vimentin promoter reporter line, use morpholino knockdown and F0 CRISPR mosaics to demonstrate vimentin's requirement for regeneration, employ long-duration multiphoton and second harmonic generation (SHG) imaging to visualize collagen-epithelial dynamics in vivo, and develop a burn wound model to further test the role of collagen integrity. The conceptual advance—that collagen fibers serve as physical guides for epithelial regrowth during regeneration, regulated by a ROS-vimentin axis—is novel and potentially significant for understanding wound repair mechanisms.

**Strengths**
- The live, long-duration SHG imaging combined with epithelial reporters to visualize collagen fiber–epithelial cell dynamics during wound healing is technically impressive and, to the authors' knowledge, unprecedented in vivo. This approach reveals a compelling spatial and temporal association between extending SHG fibers and epithelial projections.
- The generation of the Tg(−2vim:egfp) reporter line is a useful tool for the field, validated against in situ hybridization and EMT induction by oncogenic Ras.
- Multiple loss-of-function approaches for vimentin (two independent morpholinos, RNA rescue, F0 CRISPR mosaics) strengthen the conclusion that vimentin is required for fin regeneration, consistent with mammalian studies.
- The conceptual framework linking early ROS signaling → vimentin expression → collagen regulation → mechanical guidance of epithelial regrowth is a novel and integrative model that connects early damage signals to the physical processes of tissue repair.
- The burn wound model is creative and provides an orthogonal approach to test the importance of collagen fiber integrity for projection formation and regeneration.
- Quantitative analyses using CurveAlign software for fiber orientation and contour measurements add rigor to the SHG imaging observations.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**
- **Lack of stable vimentin mutants**: The primary genetic evidence relies on morpholino knockdown and F0 CRISPR mosaics, both of which have well-documented limitations (off-target effects for morpholinos; variable mosaicism for F0 injections). The authors have validated germline transmission of CRISPR mutations but do not present data from stable homozygous mutants. Stable mutant analysis is important to confirm the regeneration phenotype with confidence.
- **Withaferin A specificity**: The authors acknowledge that WA directly binds and disrupts vimentin filaments in addition to inhibiting NFκB. This dual activity makes it impossible to attribute WA's effects specifically to NFκB inhibition. Genetic inhibition of NFκB (e.g., dominant-negative IκB or morpholino targeting NFκB components) would be necessary to establish the ROS→NFκB→vimentin pathway with confidence.
- **Identity of wound-edge vim+ cells remains unknown**: The authors exclude epithelial cells and macrophages but do not positively identify the vim+ wound cells. Co-staining with fibroblast markers, mesenchymal markers, or lineage tracing would substantially strengthen the mechanistic model. Without knowing the cell identity, it is difficult to understand how intracellular vimentin in these cells regulates extracellular collagen fiber organization.
- **Causation vs. correlation for collagen guidance**: The claim that collagen fibers "guide" or "push" epithelial projections is largely inferred from spatial and temporal co-occurrence in live imaging. Alternative interpretations—such as epithelial cells pulling on collagen fibers, or both responding independently to a common signal—are not excluded. The BAPN and burn experiments are supportive but have significant caveats: BAPN is toxic at later stages (limiting analysis), and burn injury disrupts far more than collagen alone (thermal damage to cells, altered inflammation, tissue necrosis), making it difficult to attribute effects specifically to collagen loss.
- **DPI specificity and ROS pathway**: DPI inhibits flavoenzymes broadly, not just DUOX-derived wound ROS. While the DUOX morpholino partially addresses this, the 1-hour pre/post treatment window still affects many ROS-dependent processes. The paper would benefit from more specific genetic tools to isolate wound-induced H₂O₂ signaling.
- **Collagen expression data interpretation**: The qRT-PCR from pooled fin tissue cannot distinguish whether reduced collagen expression in vimentin morphants reflects a direct vimentin-Larp6 mechanism or secondary effects (e.g., fewer mesenchymal cells, altered wound signaling). In situ hybridization for col1a1/col2a1b in vimentin-depleted vs. control wounds would help localize expression changes.
- **Reproducibility of some experiments**: Several key observations (e.g., oncogenic Ras induction of vimentin reporter, Figure 1H-I; SHG/Col II co-labeling, Figure 6A-B; burn wound vim+ cell morphology, Figure 7F) are reported as "observed in two experiments" or "two replicates" without formal quantification or statistical testing. These should be performed with larger sample sizes and quantified.
- **Projection scoring subjectivity**: The binary scoring of projections (present/absent) from bright-field images is subjective and could benefit from blinded scoring and more quantitative metrics (e.g., number of projections per fin, projection length, or automated detection).

**Minor Comments**
- The paper would benefit from a clear schematic model figure summarizing the proposed ROS→NFκB→vimentin→collagen→epithelial projection pathway, with indication of which steps are directly demonstrated vs. inferred.
- Figure 2J: The ratio-based quantification of vim:egfp intensity is reasonable but the sample size (n=12 over 3 replicates with 2–6 fins per treatment per replicate) is modest. Increasing the number of animals per replicate would strengthen this key measurement.
- The Larp6 morpholino experiment (Figure 6—figure supplement 1C,D) shows reduced col1a1 but not col2a1b expression, and reduced regenerate area but not length. The authors note this suggests "multiple pathways" but do not discuss what additional mechanisms might link vimentin to col2a1b regulation or to regenerate length.
- The term "projections" is used for both epithelial cell extensions and collagen fiber extensions, which can be confusing. Clearer terminology would help readability.
- Several supplemental videos are referenced but their descriptions in the text could be more specific about what the reader should observe.
- The discussion of translational relevance to human wound healing is speculative and could be toned down given the substantial differences between zebrafish fin regeneration and mammalian cutaneous wound repair.

**Recommendation**
Major Revisions
The study presents a novel and interesting conceptual model supported by creative imaging approaches, but the central mechanistic pathway (particularly the NFκB link and the causal role of collagen in guiding epithelial projections) requires stronger genetic evidence and more rigorous controls to meet eLife's standards for a convincing mechanistic advance.
