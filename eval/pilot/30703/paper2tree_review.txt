**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** contribution by connecting wound-induced ROS signaling to vimentin expression, collagen reorganization, and epithelial projection formation during zebrafish fin regeneration. However, the evidence supporting the central mechanistic chain is **incomplete** at several critical nodes—particularly the NFκB link, the causal role of collagen projections in guiding epithelial growth, and the collagen-specific perturbation experiments—preventing the claims from reaching the standard required for a fully convincing mechanistic model.

**Summary**

This paper proposes a signaling cascade in which wound-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS) activate NFκB, which drives vimentin expression in mesenchymal cells at the wound edge, which in turn regulates collagen expression and reorganization into dynamic projections that physically guide epithelial cell extensions during zebrafish larval caudal fin regeneration. The authors generate a novel Tg(−2vim:egfp) vimentin reporter line, use pharmacological (DPI, Withaferin A, BAPN) and genetic (morpholino, CRISPR F0 mosaic) perturbations to interrogate each node of the pathway, and employ an impressive combination of live confocal, second harmonic generation (SHG), and LC-PolScope imaging to visualize collagen fiber dynamics and epithelial cell behavior simultaneously in vivo. The study further introduces a burn wound model as a tool to disrupt collagen integrity and compare healing outcomes. While the overall framework connecting ROS → vimentin → collagen → epithelial projections → regeneration is supported by convergent evidence at several nodes, key links in the chain rest on confounded pharmacology, correlative imaging, or experiments with significant alternative interpretations.

**Strengths**

- **Vimentin is required for caudal fin regeneration (high support):** The convergence of two independent morpholinos, CRISPR F0 mosaics, and RNA rescue provides robust evidence that vimentin is necessary for efficient wound healing. Large sample sizes (n=85–130 per group), high statistical significance (p<0.0001), and the demonstration that developmental fin growth is unaffected all strengthen this conclusion considerably.

- **Wound-induced vimentin expression at the wound edge (high support):** Both whole-mount in situ hybridization and live reporter imaging independently confirm localized vimentin induction by 24 hpw, with a clear temporal progression documented by time-lapse confocal microscopy. The sense probe control and complementary methods make this a well-supported observation.

- **Vimentin depletion reduces collagen expression (high support):** qRT-PCR demonstrating reduced col1a1 (p=0.004) and col2a1b (p=0.0404) in vimentin-depleted larvae, combined with SHG quantification showing reduced contour length (p=0.0009) and altered fiber angle (p=0.0003), provides strong quantitative evidence that vimentin regulates both collagen expression and fiber organization at the wound.

- **Collagen fibers are physically present within epithelial projections (high support):** The use of four complementary imaging modalities (bright field, LC-PolScope, SHG, and phalloidin staining) to demonstrate fibrillar structures within projections, with visible cell membrane stretching around fibers, represents compelling correlative evidence of a physical relationship between collagen fibers and epithelial extensions.

- **SHG fibers contain multiple collagen types (high support):** The dual SHG and anti-type II collagen immunofluorescence showing co-association, including the novel observation of Col II labeling at broken SHG fiber tips, adds important compositional information about the wound-edge matrix.

- **CurveAlign fiber orientation analysis (high support):** The use of an established, unbiased computational tool (CurveAlign) to quantify fiber angles provides objective evidence that vimentin depletion alters collagen fiber organization, moving fibers from a perpendicular to a more parallel orientation relative to the wound edge.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **The NFκB link relies entirely on Withaferin A, which has an unresolvable confound (low support, claim c1.2.5):** The authors themselves acknowledge that Withaferin A directly inhibits vimentin protein. This makes it impossible to distinguish whether the observed reduction in vimentin reporter activity reflects NFκB inhibition or direct vimentin destabilization. No genetic NFκB perturbation (e.g., dominant-negative IκB, p65 morpholino, or the available Tg(nfκb:egfp) reporter line) is employed. Without an independent, specific manipulation of NFκB, this node of the proposed pathway remains unsupported. The authors should either provide genetic evidence for NFκB involvement or substantially qualify this aspect of their model.

- **The claim that collagen projections "guide" or "push" epithelial cells is correlative, not causal (medium support, claim c1.5):** While the live dual imaging of SHG fibers and Tg(krt4:egfp-caax) is technically impressive, no experiment directly tests whether collagen fibers drive epithelial extension. The temporal resolution (~15 min intervals) cannot determine which extends first. No direct manipulation of collagen projections independent of vimentin/ROS is presented (e.g., laser ablation of individual fibers). The language "guide" and "push" implies a mechanistic directionality that exceeds what the imaging data can support. This central claim requires either direct mechanical perturbation experiments or significant qualification of the interpretive language throughout the manuscript.

- **BAPN experiments are severely confounded by toxicity (low support, claim c1.8.1):** BAPN causes lethality between 4–5 dpf, and the authors acknowledge that "it is possible that the observed effects on regeneration were in part due to toxicity." Furthermore, the contour length paradoxically *increased* with BAPN, directly contradicting the prediction from vimentin depletion, undermining the quantification framework. BAPN also inhibits all lysyl oxidase substrates (including elastin), not just collagen. This experiment cannot be used as strong evidence for a collagen-specific role in regeneration in its current form. Dose-response experiments and comparison with less toxic collagen-specific perturbations are needed.

- **The burn wound comparison conflates collagen loss with extensive non-specific tissue damage (medium support, claim c1.8.2):** Thermal injury causes cell death, protein denaturation, vascular disruption, and altered inflammatory responses far beyond collagen fiber destruction. Comparing burn wounds to amputation wounds introduces too many confounding variables to attribute healing differences specifically to collagen fiber integrity. The different vim+ cell morphology at the burn edge further indicates fundamentally different wound biology. A rescue experiment (e.g., providing an exogenous collagen scaffold to burn wounds) would help isolate the collagen-specific contribution.

- **The identity of wound-induced vimentin-positive cells remains unresolved (low support, claim c1.2.4):** While the authors exclude epithelial cells and macrophages/leukocytes as sources, no positive identification is achieved. Without knowing what the vim+ cells are, the mechanism by which they regulate collagen expression in adjacent tissue is entirely speculative. Testing fibroblast markers, neural crest markers, or performing single-cell RNA sequencing on the wound edge would substantially strengthen the mechanistic model.

- **Projection scoring appears subjective with extremely high variability (medium support, claims c1.4.2, c1.4.3):** The 95% confidence intervals for projection frequency are unacceptably wide (e.g., vim MO: 7.56–65.78%; DMSO control: 31.76–99.58%), indicating poor assay reproducibility. No blinding of scoring is described, projection criteria are not rigorously defined, and statistical analysis uses n=3 replicate percentages rather than individual larvae. Given that projection formation is a central phenotypic readout, the authors should implement blinded scoring with defined morphometric criteria, analyze at the individual larva level, and consider quantitative metrics (number, length, width of projections) rather than binary present/absent scoring.

- **DPI specificity concerns are insufficiently addressed (required assumption across multiple claims):** DPI inhibits multiple flavin-containing enzymes beyond NADPH oxidases, including mitochondrial complex I and nitric oxide synthase. The brief treatment window (1 hr pre/post wound) mitigates but does not eliminate concerns about off-target metabolic effects. The Duox1 morpholino partially addresses this, but the Duox1 MO data is presented only qualitatively in supplementary figures without quantification. The Duox1 MO results should be quantified and presented with the same rigor as the DPI data.

- **The vimentin reporter has not been validated against endogenous protein expression (required assumption, claim c1.1):** No co-staining with anti-vimentin antibody is performed. With only 2 kb of promoter driving the reporter, regulatory elements outside this region could cause the reporter to miss or gain expression in certain contexts. GFP protein stability also differs from endogenous vimentin, potentially creating temporal artifacts. Antibody co-staining in at least the wound context would significantly strengthen confidence in the reporter.

**Minor Comments**

- The EMT validation experiment (HRasV12/G12) was performed with only two replicates and no quantification. While conceptually compelling, this should be noted as preliminary rather than definitive validation.

- The novel observation of Col II caps at broken SHG fiber tips (Figure 6—figure supplement 2) is intriguing but entirely unexplored. The authors should either investigate this further or explicitly flag it as an observation warranting future study.

- The claim of being the "first" to perform long-term in vivo SHG + epithelial cell imaging, while qualified with "to our knowledge," is difficult to verify and adds little to the scientific content. Consider removing or downplaying this priority claim.

- The Larp6 morpholino experiment (single experiment in triplicate) provides interesting but preliminary evidence for pathway branching. The partial phenocopy (area but not length) requires mechanistic explanation or explicit acknowledgment as speculative.

- Figure presentation: Some critical quantifications (Duox1 MO on vim:egfp, WA on projections) are relegated to supplementary figures. Given their importance to the proposed pathway, these should be presented with full quantification in main figures or their limitations clearly discussed.

- The slight increase in developmental fin area observed in CRISPR F0 mutants (Figure 3—figure supplement 3G) is left unexplained and could represent a confound for regeneration measurements. This should be addressed.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper addresses an interesting question with creative imaging approaches and multiple perturbation strategies, but 5 claims have low support and several central mechanistic claims (NFκB involvement, causal role of collagen projections, collagen-specific perturbations) rest on confounded pharmacology, correlative imaging, or experiments with uncontrolled variables. The strong evidence for vimentin's requirement in regeneration and its regulation of collagen expression provides a solid foundation, but the interpretive framework claiming a guided collagen-projection mechanism requires either direct causal evidence or substantial qualification before publication in eLife.
