**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a *valuable* investigation into the temporal dynamics of feedforward and feedback processing in human visual recognition using ultra-rapid serial visual presentation (RSVP) combined with MEG decoding and MEG-fMRI fusion. While the experimental design is creative and certain empirical observations are well-supported, the evidence for the central claim — that the paradigm cleanly segregates feedforward from recurrent processing and that feedback signals are localized to early visual cortex — is *incomplete*, owing to critical statistical shortcomings in the MEG-fMRI fusion results, unresolved alternative explanations, and overclaiming relative to the data.

**Summary**

The paper proposes that ultra-RSVP can be used to temporally dissociate feedforward and feedback processing in the ventral visual pathway. Participants viewed rapid streams of natural images (17 or 34 ms per picture) while MEG was recorded, and behavioral face detection was assessed. The authors report two dissociable processing stages: (1) an early bottom-up sweep with peak latencies that shift earlier at faster presentation rates, and (2) a later emergence of categorical (face vs. object) information with peak latencies that shift *later* at faster rates. This inverse relationship is argued to be inconsistent with a purely feedforward account, instead reflecting increased recurrent processing demands under challenging viewing conditions. MEG-fMRI representational similarity fusion is used to localize feedback activity to early visual cortex (EVC), where a second activation peak coincides temporally with categorical information emergence in inferior temporal cortex (IT). The authors conclude by proposing three "fundamental processing principles" of object recognition that constrain computational models requiring variable-timing recurrent architectures.

**Strengths**

- **Robust behavioral and basic neural decoding results (high support):** Face detection performance is clearly above chance in both RSVP conditions (d' = 1.95 and 3.58), and individual image decoding is significant in all three viewing conditions. This establishes that the paradigm produces meaningful neural signals amenable to analysis (claims on behavioral performance and neural decoding are well-evidenced with appropriate statistical tests).

- **Consistent early sweep dynamics across multiple metrics (high support):** Three independent measures — peak latency, onset latency, and decoding accuracy — all show consistent, graded effects across presentation rates. The peak latency shifts (121, 100, 96 ms) are statistically significant in all pairwise comparisons, and the pattern replicates in within-face and within-object analyses (Table 1), providing internal replication.

- **Temporal generalization analysis convincingly demonstrates transient dynamics (high support):** The progressive narrowing of temporal generalization matrices from 500 ms/pic to 34 ms/pic to 17 ms/pic is clearly demonstrated and consistent with suppression of sustained neural activity under RSVP, establishing a key methodological premise of the paper.

- **Categorical peak latency shifts are statistically robust (high support):** The progressive increase in categorical division peak latency (136, 169, 197 ms) across conditions, with all pairwise comparisons significant, is a compelling empirical observation. The large effect size (~60 ms range) strengthens confidence in this finding.

- **EVC-IT peak-to-peak latency increases are well-documented (high support):** The progressive increase in EVC-to-IT latency (27, 79, 115 ms) across conditions, with all pairwise comparisons significant, provides an informative quantitative characterization of how the temporal dynamics of the ventral pathway change under RSVP.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Non-significant second EVC peak at 17 ms/pic fundamentally undermines the feedback narrative:** The second EVC peak in the fastest RSVP condition — the condition where feedback should be most critical according to the authors' framework — did not reach statistical significance. Despite this, the authors mark this peak on figures and discuss it as supporting evidence for feedback. This is the single most damaging gap in the evidence chain, as the condition that should most strongly demonstrate recurrent processing fails to do so. The authors should either provide additional analyses to strengthen this evidence or substantially temper their claims about feedback at 17 ms/pic.

- **Marginal statistical support for EVC-IT temporal coincidence at 34 ms/pic (p = 0.06):** The temporal coincidence between the second EVC peak and IT peak — a centerpiece of the feedback localization argument — does not reach conventional significance for the 34 ms/pic condition. Combined with the non-significant second EVC peak at 17 ms/pic, neither RSVP condition provides conventionally significant evidence for the key claim. The authors should explicitly acknowledge this limitation and avoid presenting p = 0.06 as supporting their hypothesis without appropriate caveats.

- **Cross-cohort MEG-fMRI fusion with mismatched experimental conditions is a major concern:** The fMRI data were collected from different participants using a 500 ms/pic paradigm, not RSVP. The spatial patterns captured by fMRI under slow viewing may not accurately represent the spatial patterns of processing during ultra-RSVP. This is not merely a technical limitation — it calls into question whether the fusion analysis can validly localize RSVP-specific processes such as feedback to EVC. The authors should justify why fMRI RDMs from a fundamentally different temporal regime are valid spatial references, or ideally collect fMRI data under RSVP conditions.

- **Alternative explanations are insufficiently addressed:** The inverse relationship between early and late peak latencies — the core argument against a purely feedforward account — has plausible feedforward explanations. Degraded feedforward signals could require longer evidence accumulation at downstream stages, producing disproportionate delays without requiring feedback. Forward masking from preceding RSVP images could explain later onset latencies without invoking recurrent processing. The second EVC peak could reflect feedforward processing of post-target mask stimuli rather than target-related feedback. The authors should formally model or simulate what a purely feedforward system would predict under their specific conditions, rather than relying on the informal argument that feedforward processing "should" produce proportional delays.

- **"Fundamental processing principles" are overclaimed (low support):** Principle 2 ("delays in solving object recognition are an index of increased recurrent processing") involves circular reasoning — delays are inferred to reflect recurrence, then claimed to index recurrence. The three principles are based on a single study with 17 participants, two stimulus categories, and a binary detection task. Calling these "fundamental principles of object recognition" is not justified. This language should be substantially revised to reflect the preliminary and condition-specific nature of the evidence.

- **Internal inconsistency between claims about early activity:** The paper simultaneously argues that the first wave is "disproportionately characterized by feedforward propagating signals" and that variable onset latencies "argue against the view that the early part of the decoding time course can be uniquely tied to feedforward processing alone." These claims are in direct tension. The authors should reconcile this inconsistency and present a coherent account of what the early processing stage represents.

- **The 500 ms/pic condition comes from a different cohort with a different task:** Cross-condition comparisons involving the slow presentation rate are confounded by between-subject variability and task differences (passive viewing vs. face detection). While some findings are internally replicated within the RSVP cohort, the interpretive framework relies heavily on contrasts with the 500 ms/pic condition. This limitation should be explicitly discussed, and the authors should consider which conclusions hold when restricted to within-cohort comparisons only.

**Minor Comments**

- The onset latency for 500 ms/pic has a very wide confidence interval (9–53 ms), making it difficult to interpret quantitative comparisons with RSVP onsets. This uncertainty should be acknowledged when discussing onset latency shifts.

- No quantitative metric for "degree of transience" is provided for the temporal generalization analysis; comparisons across conditions are based on visual inspection of matrices. A formal quantitative comparison would strengthen this analysis.

- The MDS visualizations are qualitative; no quantitative clustering metric accompanies them. Consider adding a categorical separation index or silhouette score.

- The claim that results reflect "ecologically valid conditions" is somewhat weakened by the use of only 24 images (12 faces, 12 objects) and the RSVP paradigm itself, which does not correspond to normal viewing. The ecological validity language should be softened.

- Individual-level behavioral data distributions are not shown. Given that d' values at 17 ms/pic may approach chance for some participants, showing individual data would be informative.

- The discussion of neural correlates of consciousness is entirely speculative and lacks supporting data from the present study. This section should be clearly framed as speculative future direction rather than a supported conclusion.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper presents a creative paradigm and several well-supported empirical observations (26/55 claims with high support), but the central claims about feedback localization to EVC rest on statistically weak evidence (non-significant second EVC peak at 17 ms/pic; p = 0.06 for EVC-IT coincidence at 34 ms/pic), and multiple plausible alternative explanations — particularly forward masking and degraded-signal accumulation — are not adequately ruled out. The overclaiming of "fundamental principles" from limited evidence (2 low-support claims) and internal inconsistencies require substantive revision before the paper meets eLife's standards for a conceptual advance with convincing evidence.
