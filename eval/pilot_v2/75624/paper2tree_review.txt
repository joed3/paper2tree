**eLife Assessment**

This paper presents a **valuable** resource of single-cell chromatin accessibility in the developing mouse testis, with **solid** evidence supporting cell type identification and recovery of known regulatory programs, but with **incomplete** evidence for several of the more novel claims — particularly regarding novel differentiation trajectories, functional roles of identified transcription factors, and disease-relevant predictions from cross-species GWAS integration.

**Summary**

Suen et al. present a single-cell ATAC-Seq atlas of the perinatal mouse testis comprising over 25,000 cells across four developmental time points (E18.5, P0, P2, P5). By integrating chromatin accessibility data with published scRNA-Seq, the authors identify cell type-specific transcription factor (TF) regulators, cis-regulatory elements via peak-to-gene linkage, and pseudotime trajectories for both germ and somatic cell lineages. They report novel findings including a putative second germ cell differentiation trajectory potentially representing first-wave spermatogenesis, NR5A1 expression in a subset of germ cells, a previously uncharacterized Sertoli cell subpopulation (SC1), heterogeneity within Leydig and PTM/stromal compartments, and the connection of GWAS variants for testosterone levels and coronary artery disease to specific testicular cell types and candidate target genes. The study aims to provide a regulatory "blueprint" of the male germline and its somatic microenvironment during a critical developmental window.

**Strengths**

- **Robust cell type identification from chromatin accessibility (Primary claim c1.1; strong evidence, calibrated):** The study demonstrates that scATAC-Seq can reliably resolve testicular cell types through multiple convergent lines of evidence — gene activity scores of known markers, integration with independent scRNA-Seq data yielding high prediction scores, correspondence of DARs with marker genes, and cross-platform validation against bulk Sertoli cell DNase-Seq from Maatouk et al. (2017). The 214,890 accessible chromatin regions and stringent QC of 25,613 cells provide a high-quality foundational dataset.

- **Principled TF identification framework addressing motif ambiguity (Supporting claim on motif similarity; strong evidence, calibrated):** The authors transparently acknowledge the false discovery problem from TF family motif similarity (exemplified by SOX9/SOX3) and implement a principled solution by correlating chromVAR motif activity with gene expression. Recovery of well-established regulators — GATA1 in Sertoli cells (with triple concordance of motif activity, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression), DMRT1 in germ cells, NR5A1/GATA4 in Leydig/Sertoli cells — validates this multi-layered analytical approach.

- **Peak-to-gene linkage validated by known enhancers (Supporting claim on TESCO/Wt1 recovery; moderate-strong evidence):** The identification of 35,245 peak-to-gene links with 3,262 overlapping independently defined testis enhancers (Gao and Qian, 2020), and the successful recovery of the well-characterized TESCO enhancer linked to Sox9 in Sertoli cells, provides important positive controls for the regulatory element predictions.

- **DLK1 localization finding with experimental validation (Supporting claim on DLK1; strong evidence, calibrated):** The demonstration that DLK1 is expressed in stromal cells and PTMs rather than primarily in Leydig cells — supported by convergent scATAC-Seq data, FACS sorting with RT-PCR (p<0.001, n=3), and immunostaining — represents a well-validated finding that challenges the use of DLK1 as an immature Leydig cell marker, at least in mouse.

- **Biologically coherent GWAS positive controls (Supporting claim on Leydig-testosterone enrichment; strong evidence, calibrated):** The LD score regression analysis correctly identifies Leydig cell enrichment for testosterone-level GWAS heritability, with specific variants (rs4919685, rs743572) at the known CYP17A1 locus, and appropriate negative controls (no enrichment for Parkinson's, depression) demonstrate the framework's biological specificity.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **The claim that scATAC-Seq identifies TFs "that cannot be adequately identified from scRNA-Seq alone" is asserted but not demonstrated (Primary claim c1.2; moderate evidence, overclaimed).** No systematic comparison between TFs discoverable by scRNA-Seq versus those uniquely revealed by the chromatin accessibility approach is provided. The authors should either perform this comparison or substantially temper this claim. Additionally, the conclusion that cell type-specific TFs "likely regulate chromatin accessibility" (Supporting claim c1.2.5) represents a correlation-to-causation leap — no perturbation experiments are performed. The authors should clearly frame all TF–chromatin relationships as correlative and discuss the limitations of inferring causation from observational data.

- **The second germ cell differentiation trajectory and its interpretation as first-wave spermatogenesis rest on insufficient evidence (Primary claim c1.4; moderate evidence, overclaimed).** This trajectory is a computational inference from snapshot data without lineage tracing, and only one feature — low Ngn3 gene activity — supports the first-wave interpretation. Gene activity scores from scATAC-Seq are an indirect proxy for expression, and the "unknown" populations could represent doublets, technical artifacts, or transitional states. The authors should either provide additional markers distinguishing first-wave from conventional spermatogonial differentiation, or substantially downgrade the certainty of this interpretation. If possible, validation using an independent dataset or experimental approach (e.g., Ngn3-reporter mice) would strengthen this claim.

- **Novel cis-regulatory elements are entirely computational predictions without experimental validation (Primary claim c1.3 and supporting claims on Sox9 novel elements; moderate evidence).** The three novel Sox9 regulatory elements and other predicted CREs are generated from peak-to-gene correlations within a 250 kb window, but no reporter assays, CRISPR deletions, or chromatin conformation capture experiments are performed. The false positive rate of the 250 kb correlation-based approach is neither assessed nor discussed. The authors should at minimum estimate false positive rates (e.g., through permutation analyses) and explicitly acknowledge this limitation when presenting specific novel CRE predictions.

- **The GWAS integration claims are overclaimed given the fundamental cross-species gap (Primary claim c1.6; moderate evidence, overclaimed).** While the cross-species limitation is acknowledged in the discussion, the claim language throughout implies greater predictive power than warranted. The Sertoli cell–coronary artery disease connection is particularly speculative, relying on an untested multi-step chain of reasoning (Sertoli cells → testosterone → CAD). Additionally, specific GWAS examples (e.g., rs2400899 in Wars linked to Slc25a47; rs941576 in Meg3 linked to Dlk1 in the context of T1D) lack mechanistic explanation for why a variant in one gene would regulate another, and no experimental validation is provided. The authors should recalibrate the language to consistently present these as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive predictions, and discuss multiple testing across many trait–cell type combinations.

- **The NR5A1 finding in germ cells does not definitively rule out technical artifacts (Supporting claim c1.4.4; moderate evidence, overclaimed).** Co-localization of TRA98 and NR5A1 by immunostaining demonstrates that some germ cells express NR5A1 protein, but the claim that this "ruled out the possible contamination from somatic cells" in the scATAC-Seq data is overclaimed. Doublets in scATAC-Seq would not be detectable by tissue immunostaining. No quantification of the fraction of NR5A1+ germ cells is provided. The authors should temper this claim and provide quantification.

- **Novel somatic cell subpopulations require stronger evidence to exclude over-clustering artifacts (Primary claim c1.5; moderate evidence, calibrated but with significant caveats).** The SC1 Sertoli cell subpopulation, while supported by MBD3/AMH immunostaining heterogeneity, lacks information about cell numbers per sub-cluster, statistical power, and robustness to clustering parameter choices. The 10 PTM/stromal clusters from what is likely a modest cell number raise concerns about over-clustering, and the telocyte-like identity of PC2 is based solely on marker gene activity. The authors should report cell numbers per sub-cluster, perform clustering stability analyses (e.g., bootstrapping), and discuss the risk of over-fragmentation.

- **Biological variability and technical reproducibility cannot be assessed.** Pooling three mice per time point and processing all samples in a single batch prevents assessment of inter-individual variation and technical reproducibility. This should be explicitly discussed as a limitation, and the authors should comment on how this affects confidence in rare populations and subtle sub-clustering results.

**Minor Comments**

- The per-time-point breakdown of cell numbers after QC should be explicitly stated in the main text to allow assessment of temporal coverage balance.
- For the homeobox gene enrichment finding in PTMs (9 of 46 TFs), a statistical test against background expectation should be performed.
- The PGR motif enrichment in PTMs is noted but its biological significance is never discussed — this should be either interpreted or noted as requiring further investigation.
- The word "comprehensive" in describing the atlas overstates its scope, given coverage of only four perinatal time points without adult stages or complementary epigenetic marks; "extensive" or "detailed" would be more appropriate.
- Specific correlation values for key individual TFs (e.g., GATA1, DMRT1) identified as positive regulators should be reported in the text or a supplementary table for transparency.
- The Id4 "motif binding activity" description needs clarification, as Id4 is a transcriptional inhibitor lacking a canonical DNA-binding domain — the authors should specify whether this refers to E-box motifs.
- Figure legends should provide quantification for immunostaining results (e.g., percentage of TRA98+ cells co-expressing NR5A1; proportions of MBD3/AMH expression patterns in Sertoli cells).

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

While the dataset is a valuable resource and the core cell type identification and TF recovery are well-supported (23/62 claims with high support, 0 with low support), several of the paper's most novel and prominent claims — the second germ cell trajectory, the functional interpretation of identified TFs, novel CREs, and cross-species GWAS predictions — are overclaimed relative to the evidence provided, requiring either additional experimental validation, computational robustness analyses, or substantial recalibration of claim language to meet eLife's standard that central claims be well-supported by the data.
