**eLife Assessment**
This useful study provides a single-cell chromatin accessibility atlas of the developing mouse testis, generating a resource that catalogs cell type-specific regulatory elements and transcription factor activities across perinatal stages. While the computational framework is solid, the central biological claims — including novel germ cell trajectories, previously uncharacterized TF regulators, and GWAS target cell type predictions — rest on incomplete evidence, as they largely lack experimental validation and are subject to methodological caveats that weaken confidence in the conclusions.

**Summary**
The authors apply scATAC-seq (Bio-Rad ddSEQ platform) to profile chromatin accessibility in ~25,600 cells from mouse testes at four perinatal time points (E18.5, P0, P2, P5). By integrating with a previously published scRNA-seq dataset, they annotate cell types, identify cell type-specific transcription factors through correlation of chromatin motif accessibility with gene expression, predict cis-regulatory element–gene linkages, and perform GWAS enrichment analyses to nominate target cell types for disease-associated variants (testosterone levels, male infertility, coronary artery disease). Key biological claims include (1) identification of a second germ cell differentiation trajectory potentially representing the first wave of spermatogenesis, (2) discovery of NR5A1-expressing germ cell subpopulations, (3) characterization of a novel Sertoli cell subpopulation (SC1), and (4) enrichment of coronary artery disease heritability in Sertoli cell-specific peaks. The study is presented as a resource, with an accompanying web atlas.

**Strengths**
- The dataset is well-powered for a developmental scATAC-seq study, with over 25,000 cells spanning four informative time points that capture key transitions in testicular development, including gonocyte-to-spermatogonia conversion and somatic cell maturation.
- The analytical framework is comprehensive and follows current best practices: the authors employ iterative LSI dimensionality reduction, chromVAR motif deviation scoring, peak-to-gene linkage analysis, and pseudotime trajectory reconstruction, providing a multi-layered view of regulatory dynamics.
- The strategy of identifying "positive TF regulators" by requiring concordance between motif accessibility and gene expression/gene activity scores is a principled approach that reduces false positives inherent to motif-only analyses, and successfully recovers known regulators (e.g., DMRT1 in germ cells, SOX9 in Sertoli cells, AR in PTMs).
- The peak-to-gene linkage analysis recovers known enhancers (e.g., TESCO for Sox9, Wt1 enhancer), lending credibility to the approach and providing a catalog of candidate regulatory elements for future functional studies.
- The public web resource and provision of supplementary tables enhance the utility of this work for the reproductive biology community.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**
- **Lack of biological replicates for scATAC-seq**: Cells from three mice per time point were pooled into a single library. Without independent biological replicates, it is impossible to assess reproducibility of the identified cell clusters, DARs, or TF activities. This is a fundamental limitation that should be explicitly discussed, and ideally addressed with at least one replicate for a key time point.

- **Cross-study integration introduces uncertainty**: The reliance on a separately generated scRNA-seq dataset (GSE130593) for cell type annotation and gene expression correlation is a significant caveat. Batch effects, differences in cell dissociation protocols, and platform-specific biases could distort the integration. The authors should quantify integration quality beyond prediction scores (e.g., assess label transfer accuracy with known markers, compare cell type proportions between modalities) and discuss how this limitation might affect peak-to-gene links and TF regulator identification.

- **The "unknown" germ cell populations and second trajectory are insufficiently supported**: The claim that a second trajectory may represent the first wave of spermatogenesis is intriguing but speculative. The evidence rests on low Ngn3 gene activity scores in scATAC-seq, which is an indirect proxy. The "unknown" clusters express somatic markers (NR5A1), and while the authors show NR5A1 immunostaining in some germ cells, they do not rule out that these clusters arise from doublets or integration artifacts (e.g., misassigned anchors transferring somatic labels). The authors should (a) systematically assess doublet scores specifically in these clusters, (b) show co-staining with additional germ cell markers, and (c) provide more rigorous evidence (e.g., proportion of NR5A1+ germ cells, quantification) before proposing a biological trajectory model.

- **Limited experimental validation of novel TF predictions**: The study's primary contribution is identifying candidate TF regulators for each cell type, yet virtually none are experimentally validated. For a study aiming to advance understanding of gene regulatory networks, at least one or two key predictions should be tested — for example, by examining the effect of a predicted TF's perturbation on target gene accessibility or expression in primary cells or organoids.

- **GWAS enrichment analysis requires more rigorous treatment**: Applying LD score regression with mouse peaks lifted to human coordinates involves assumptions about regulatory conservation that are not validated. The authors should (a) describe the liftover procedure and report the proportion of peaks successfully converted, (b) provide enrichment results with confidence intervals or permutation-based p-values, (c) include negative control traits more systematically, and (d) temper conclusions about human disease mechanisms (e.g., the CAD–Sertoli cell link) given the cross-species nature of the analysis.

- **Peak-to-gene links lack validation beyond overlap with known enhancers**: The 35,245 peak-to-gene links are entirely correlative. While overlap with known testis enhancers is encouraging, the authors should consider orthogonal validation — for example, comparison with published Hi-C or promoter capture Hi-C data from testis — or at minimum discuss the false discovery rate expected from their correlation thresholds.

- **Sertoli cell SC1 characterization is preliminary**: The claim that SC1 represents a less differentiated Sertoli cell state is based on lower Sox9/Dmrt1/Wt1 activity and higher Amh/Mbd3, with limited immunostaining. Alternative explanations (e.g., SC1 cells are at a different cell cycle phase, or represent a technical artifact from over-clustering) should be explored. Cell cycle scoring and assessment of cluster stability (e.g., bootstrapping) would strengthen this finding.

**Minor Comments**
- The paper would benefit from clearer figure labeling; several panels are referenced in the text but require navigating multiple supplementary figures, making it difficult to follow the analytical flow.
- The fragment size distribution (Figure 1—figure supplement 1B) and QC metrics should be shown per sample rather than in aggregate to assess consistency across time points.
- The authors state that they used Harmony for the global analysis but not for within-cell-type re-clustering. The rationale for this choice should be better justified, as developmental stage effects could still confound sub-clustering.
- The web resource URL uses an AWS S3 bucket that may not be permanently maintained. The authors should consider archiving the data in a stable repository.
- Some TF family members (e.g., SP/KLF, NR) are discussed as cell type-specific regulators, but the motif similarity issue acknowledged for SOX9/SOX3 likely applies broadly. A more systematic assessment of which motifs can be confidently distinguished would be valuable.
- The RT-qPCR validation of DLK1+ sorted cells (Figure 3F) uses only three markers and does not include a Leydig cell-negative marker to confirm the absence of Leydig cell contamination, despite the relevance to human DLK1 as a Leydig cell marker.

**Recommendation**
Major Revisions
The study presents a useful resource but the key biological claims — novel germ cell trajectories, cell type-specific TF networks, and GWAS target cell predictions — require substantially more rigorous validation, both computational (doublet analysis, integration quality assessment, cross-species liftover details) and experimental (functional testing of at least one TF prediction), to meet eLife's standard of evidence supporting the central conclusions.
