# v1.7-vs-v2.0 Evaluation Report

**Sample:** 19 eLife papers (20 sampled, seed 42; paper 45562 excluded from both arms — the shared claim extractor fails on it persistently). Reviews scored against the human eLife review. Opus at $5/$25 per MTok. Total API spend: **$53.06** (v1.7 $24.10 + v2.0 $28.96; baseline reused across versions).

## Headline

**Neither pre-registered criterion holds.** On prose-similarity to human reviews, the compositional v2.0 DAG pipeline (a) does **not** beat the single-prompt baseline, and (b) does **not** improve over v1.7 — and on concern-precision it *regresses* significantly. Per the §14 decision rule, this is the "neither holds" branch: **pause and rethink the judgment layer rather than build more features.**

## Criterion 1 — DAG vs. single-prompt baseline (paired, n=19)

The DAG still does not beat a single well-prompted call; it is significantly *behind* on concern precision/F1.

Compositional evaluation did not move the prose metrics; it regressed concern precision.

## Cost / latency (the "worth it?" tax)

| Arm | s/paper | tokens (in+out) | $/paper | total (19) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 71 | 16k + 3k | $0.15 | $2.86 |
| v1.7 DAG | 599 | 55k + 34k | $1.12 | $21.25 |
| v2.0 DAG | 764 | 111k + 39k | $1.52 | $28.96 |

v2.0 costs **+36%** and runs **+28%** slower than v1.7 (the child-evaluation context roughly doubles input tokens), for no prose-metric gain. Both DAG arms are ~7–10× the baseline's cost.

## Crucial caveats (why this is "rethink", not "v2 is bad")

1. **These metrics measure similarity to one human review — a weak proxy** for judgment quality, and they cannot see what v2 actually changed: compositional catching of *inferential* gaps, the `claim_evidence_calibration` (overclaiming) axis, or the interactive artifact. The §14 metrics that *would* test those (overclaiming-detection agreement, thesis-verdict correctness) require hand-labeling and were **not** run.
2. **The concern-precision regression has a plausible benign cause:** v2 replaced the adversarial "Reviewer 2" persona with a calibrated one ("do not manufacture concerns"). Fewer/softer concerns overlap less with the human's specific weakness sentences — this metric penalizes exactly the manufactured-concern reduction we intended. Whether that's a real regression or a metric artifact is untestable with concern-alignment alone.

## Recommendation (per §14 decision rule)

- **Do not proceed with Phases 4–5** (type rubrics, extraction hardening) on the strength of prose metrics — they show no lift.
- **Build the judgment-specific metrics first** (overclaiming agreement, thesis-verdict correctness on constructed cases) and re-decide. If v2 wins there, its value is real but invisible to prose similarity; if not, the compositional bet needs rethinking.
- **The interactive artifact remains the defensible value** regardless — it was never in scope for these metrics.
- Consider whether the calibrated persona should be re-tuned, or whether concern-precision is simply the wrong target.
