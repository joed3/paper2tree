**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** investigation into post-translational modifications of GFAP as potential markers of Alexander disease severity, employing a multi-modal experimental approach spanning patient tissue, iPSC-derived astrocytes, and cell-based reconstitution systems. The evidence is **incomplete**, as the central thesis linking Ser13 phosphorylation and caspase-6 cleavage into a coordinated PTM "signature" driving defective GFAP proteostasis is overclaimed relative to the correlative nature of the data, the small sample sizes, and the inability of interventional experiments (caspase inhibition, cleavage-site mutagenesis) to resolve GFAP aggregation.

**Summary**

This paper investigates whether post-translational modifications on GFAP serve as molecular markers linking different AxD-causing mutations to disease severity. Using mass spectrometry of post-mortem brain tissue from 13 AxD patients harboring 10 different mutations, the authors identify phosphorylation at Ser13 as selectively elevated in patients who died young (median age 1.7 years) compared to those who survived longer (median age 38 years). They demonstrate that phospho-mimic GFAP mutants (S13D/S13E) form aggregates in cells and are incapable of filament assembly in vitro, and that iPSC-derived astrocytes carrying the R239C mutation accumulate pSer13-GFAP in perinuclear aggregates associated with nuclear invaginations. The authors further report that caspase-6 expression and caspase-6-mediated GFAP cleavage at Asp225 are selectively elevated in young AxD patients and correlate with pSer13 levels, proposing a novel PTM signature for infantile AxD.

The study employs an ambitious experimental strategy combining proteomics, phospho-specific antibodies, in vitro reconstitution, overexpression in SW13vim⁻ cells, CRISPR-corrected iPSC-derived astrocytes, and post-mortem immunohistochemistry. While the convergence of multiple approaches is commendable, the root claim of a mechanistically linked PTM "signature" associated with defective proteostasis exceeds what the data demonstrate, as the authors themselves acknowledge that causality has not been established and that interventional experiments failed to resolve aggregation.

**Strengths**

- **Caspase-6 as a severity marker**: The binary detection of caspase-6 in all 8 young AxD patients versus essentially undetectable levels in 5 older patients, combined with age-matched non-AxD controls ruling out a developmental confound (p<0.01, two-way ANOVA), constitutes one of the paper's most robust findings. The consistency across all young patients is striking.

- **In vitro GFAP assembly data**: The demonstration that purified S13D-GFAP is completely incapable of filament formation, instead producing homogeneous globular structures, provides strong mechanistic evidence that Ser13 phosphorylation fundamentally alters GFAP assembly properties. The contrast with cell-based aggregation is honestly reported and informative.

- **Isogenic iPSC controls**: The generation and thorough characterization of CRISPR/Cas9-corrected isogenic control iPSC lines, including pluripotency validation, karyotyping, and off-target analysis, represents rigorous experimental design. The significantly elevated GFAP in mutant versus isogenic control astrocytes (p<0.0001) is well-supported and consistent with in vivo data.

- **Orthogonal validation of pSer13 elevation**: The convergence of mass spectrometry and KT13 phospho-specific immunoblot on the same conclusion about pSer13 elevation in young AxD patients strengthens internal consistency.

- **D225E cleavage-site mutagenesis**: The >90% reduction in GFAP cleavage (p<0.0001) confirms the specific cleavage site, and the caspase-6 inhibitor data (p<0.0001 for S13D/R79H) provides complementary pharmacological evidence.

- **Transparent reporting of limitations**: The authors' honest acknowledgment that overexpression in SW13vim⁻ cells may not reflect astrocyte biology, that the iPSC aggregates are not identical to electron-dense Rosenthal fibers, and that causality between phosphorylation and cleavage is not established, reflects scientific integrity.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **The root claim of a coordinated PTM "signature" is overclaimed**: The term "signature" implies mechanistic coordination between Ser13 phosphorylation and caspase-6 cleavage, but the paper explicitly cannot determine whether these are causally related or independent stress markers. Critically, the in vitro data show S13D prevents filament formation without causing aggregation, while blocking caspase cleavage (D225E mutagenesis or Ac-VEID-CHO) failed to resolve aggregate formation in cells. These negative interventional results undermine the mechanistic coherence of the proposed signature. The authors should either provide additional evidence linking these PTMs mechanistically or substantially revise the framing to present them as correlative markers that co-occur with severe disease.

- **Small sample sizes limit the robustness of the central patient-tissue findings**: The key comparisons rely on 8 young versus 5 older AxD patients and 3 non-AxD controls for mass spectrometry. With acknowledged outliers in both age groups (Figure 2B, lanes 3 and 11), the p<0.05 findings are fragile. The claim of "selective" elevation of pSer13 in young patients should be tempered to "preferential" or "predominant," and the authors should discuss whether the outliers might track with specific mutations, genetic modifiers, or post-mortem variables.

- **Increased cleaved GFAP in iPSC-astrocytes lacks statistical support**: This claim (increased cleaved GFAP in AxD versus isogenic control iPSC-astrocytes) is rated as overclaimed with weak evidence. No statistical analysis is reported, replicates are not stated, and loading different total protein amounts to normalize GFAP monomer levels is methodologically unusual and could introduce artifacts. This finding needs proper quantification with biological replicates and statistical testing.

- **pSer13 detection in isogenic control astrocytes undermines disease specificity**: The presence of pSer13 signal in control iPSC-astrocytes, attributed speculatively to culture conditions, is problematic because the patient-tissue data claim disease specificity. This discrepancy needs experimental investigation — for example, testing whether kinase inhibitors eliminate pSer13 in controls, or whether passage number or culture stress modulates this signal. Without this, the disease-specificity of pSer13 as a marker is weakened.

- **Generalizability limited to a single iPSC mutation**: All iPSC-astrocyte experiments model only R239C, yet the paper's conclusions are framed as broadly applicable across >70 AxD mutations. At minimum, the authors should explicitly discuss this limitation prominently and consider whether additional mutations should be modeled to support their generalizable claims.

- **Ac-VEID-CHO specificity concerns**: The caspase-6 inhibitor used is known to inhibit other caspases. Genetic approaches (caspase-6 siRNA/shRNA knockdown or CRISPR knockout) would provide more specific evidence. At minimum, the authors should discuss the specificity limitations of this inhibitor and present dose-response data.

- **Speculative biomarker and therapeutic claims lack any supporting data**: The suggestions that pSer13-GFAP could be monitored in CSF/blood and that kinase inhibition could be therapeutic are presented without any experimental data. While appropriately phrased as possibilities, these claims should be either supported with preliminary data (e.g., detectability of pSer13 in biofluids) or relegated to a brief mention in the discussion rather than featured prominently.

- **Missing statistical analyses for several key comparisons**: Statistical tests are absent for SW13vim⁻ cell quantification (Figure 3B), hmm GFAP oligomer comparison between AxD and isogenic control iPSC-astrocytes, and the proportion of cells showing different aggregate phenotypes (Types I-III). These quantifications require proper statistical treatment.

**Minor Comments**

- The 5% FDR cutoff used for peptide identification is more liberal than the standard 1% commonly used in phosphoproteomics studies. The authors should justify this choice or report results at 1% FDR.

- EM quantification (number of cells examined, frequency of nuclear invaginations and fibrous bundles) should be reported to support claims about the prevalence of these structures.

- The broad age ranges defining "young" (0.5–14 years) and "old" (27–50 years) groups leave a 13–27 year gap with no data. The authors should discuss whether a continuous analysis of pSer13 versus age-at-death (rather than a binary grouping) would be informative.

- Colocalization of caspase-6 with GFAP aggregates in iPSC-astrocytes is described but not quantified (e.g., Pearson's or Manders' coefficients). Quantitative colocalization analysis would strengthen this observation.

- The manuscript would benefit from a clear table or figure summarizing which findings were replicated across which experimental systems (SW13vim⁻, iPSC-astrocytes, patient tissue) to help readers assess the convergence of evidence.

- The S16/S17 phosphorylation findings are only from SW13vim⁻ cells and are not validated in astrocytes or patient tissue. This limitation should be stated more prominently.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper addresses an important question in a rare neurodegenerative disease and presents several well-supported individual findings, but the central thesis of a coordinated PTM signature is overclaimed relative to the available evidence. Of 51 claims, 13 have high support while 4 have low support, and the root claim is rated as overclaimed. Critical revisions are needed: (1) recalibrating the framing from a mechanistic "signature" to correlative markers, (2) providing statistical support for key iPSC-astrocyte comparisons, (3) addressing the disease-specificity concern raised by pSer13 detection in control astrocytes, and (4) strengthening the caspase-6 specificity data with genetic approaches or dose-response experiments.
