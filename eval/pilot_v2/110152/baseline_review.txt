**eLife Assessment**
This valuable study provides solid-to-convincing structural and biophysical evidence defining the residue-level determinants of partner specificity within CTLH-CRA dimerization interfaces; however, the central claim that these specificity codes can be exploited to rationally reprogram ring assembly and thereby modulate E3 ligase function rests on incomplete evidence, as no validation in the context of full-length complex reconstitution or functional ubiquitylation assays is provided.

**Summary**
The CTLH complex is a megadalton ring-shaped E3 ubiquitin ligase assembled from subunits sharing a conserved LisH-CTLH-CRA architecture, yet the molecular basis of how partner specificity is encoded at the CRA dimerization interface has remained poorly understood. This study reports nine crystal structures of mammalian CTLH-CRA domains—individually and in heterodimeric complexes—at resolutions ranging from 1.4 to 3.5 Å, filling gaps left by prior cryo-EM studies. Through systematic mutagenesis (including 52 Twa1 variants), the authors identify key aromatic, polar, and steric determinants that dictate whether a given subunit pairs with muskelin/Wdr26 or Maea. They demonstrate bidirectional specificity rewiring: a RanBP10 quadruple mutant (R10_GLFF) switches preference from muskelin to Maea, while an eight-substitution Twa1 variant (Twa1_52) acquires RanBP10-like preference for muskelin. Quantitative binding data (ITC, SEC-MALS) and competition experiments further reveal sub-nanomolar to picomolar affinities for native pairings. The authors propose these engineered variants as tools for future controlled assembly of alternative ring compositions.

**Strengths**
- **Comprehensive structural coverage:** Nine new crystal structures provide atomic-resolution views of interfaces that were unresolved or poorly resolved in prior cryo-EM maps, including the first structure of the RanBP9-muskelin CTLH-CRA interface and the previously unknown Maea homodimerization mode.
- **Rigorous bidirectional specificity swap:** The demonstration that specificity can be switched in both directions—RanBP10→Maea-binding and Twa1→muskelin-binding—is a compelling validation that the identified residues constitute a genuine specificity code rather than isolated loss-of-function observations.
- **Systematic and quantitative mutagenesis:** The screen of 52 Twa1 mutants, combined with quantitative ITC measurements (including competitive binding to extract picomolar KD values), provides a thorough dissection of the energetic contributions of individual residues. The progressive loss of muskelin binding across single, double, and triple RanBP10 mutants is particularly informative.
- **Multiple orthogonal methods:** The combination of X-ray crystallography, SEC-MALS, ITC, NAGE, and AlphaFold3 predictions across multiple species provides internally consistent and cross-validated conclusions.
- **Evolutionary perspective:** AlphaFold3 interface predictions across model organisms (yeast to vertebrates) contextualize the specificity determinants within an evolutionary framework and identify conserved versus lineage-specific features.
- **Clever experimental design:** The use of domain fusions to overcome insolubility and the competition ITC strategy to measure otherwise unfittable sub-nanomolar affinities demonstrate resourceful problem-solving.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**
- **No validation in full-length ring context:** The entire analysis is conducted with truncated CTLH-CRA domain constructs. The authors propose that specificity-switch mutants could generate alternative ring architectures (e.g., rings with extra catalytic modules or inverted RanBP9/10 orientation), but no reconstitution of a full or partial ring with these engineered subunits is attempted. At minimum, incorporation of R10_GLFF or Twa1_52 into a multi-subunit assembly (even a partial one) should be demonstrated to support the claims about reprogramming ring composition.
- **No functional assays:** The paper lacks any ubiquitylation activity data. Given that the CTLH complex is an E3 ligase, the biological relevance of interface rewiring depends on whether altered ring composition affects catalytic output. Even an in vitro ubiquitylation assay with a known substrate would substantially strengthen the manuscript's conclusions and its relevance to the E3 ligase field.
- **Construct design concerns:** The CTLH-CRA domain fusions delete internal linkers and omit entire domains (SPRY, WD40, kelch, coiled-coil). The authors acknowledge that SPRY-kelch/WD40 contacts likely contribute ~100-fold to affinity in the full-length context, yet all specificity conclusions are drawn from truncated constructs. It remains unclear whether the specificity determinants identified at the domain level are dominant or can be overridden by additional contacts in the full-length proteins.
- **Helix-swapping artifact:** The Twa1 CTLH-CRA domain crystal structure exhibits helix swapping attributed to crystal packing. While the authors argue this is unlikely in the full-length context, the use of this potentially artifactual structure to draw conclusions about interface geometry (particularly as a comparator in Figure 2C) warrants additional caution or alternative validation (e.g., solution SAXS).
- **Maea oligomerization quantification:** The concentration-dependent SEC-MALS analysis fitted with a one-site binding model to estimate a KD of ~5 μM for dimer-of-dimer formation is methodologically approximate. SEC-MALS measures weight-average molar mass across an elution peak, and the dilution occurring during chromatography complicates equilibrium analysis. Analytical ultracentrifugation (AUC) would be more appropriate for rigorous self-association quantification.
- **Limited statistical reporting:** While ITC experiments are repeated 3–4 times (6 for wild-type), standard errors are provided without explicit statement of whether these represent SEM or SD. For SEC-MALS, mass uncertainties appear to be instrument precision rather than replicate variability. Formal statistical analysis (e.g., confidence intervals for KD comparisons) would strengthen quantitative claims, particularly for the ~5-fold difference between RanBP9 and RanBP10 binding to Wdr26.
- **Speculative discussion of applications:** The Discussion extensively speculates about using CRA-interface mutations as tools for enforcing defined assembly routes, generating non-canonical architectures, and designing ring-shaped complexes. These claims go well beyond what the data support and should be tempered or moved to a separate "Future Directions" section.

**Minor Comments**
- The rationale for using murine/rat proteins rather than human orthologs should be stated more explicitly in the Results (not just Methods), along with a summary of interface residue conservation to reassure readers.
- Figure 1C is dense and would benefit from a simplified schematic showing only the topology of LisH-CRAC and CTLH-CRA interfaces, separate from the composite structural model.
- The nomenclature for RanBP10 mutants (R10_G, R10_GLFF) is introduced informally; a consistent naming convention defined early in the manuscript would aid readability.
- The Twa1 mutant numbering (Twa1_1 through Twa1_52) is difficult to follow. A summary table in the main text (not only supplementary figures) listing key mutants, their substitutions, and binding outcomes would be helpful.
- The competition ITC protocol involves removing "a few microliters" from the cell between titrations—the exact volume and its impact on concentration accuracy should be specified.
- Table 1 (crystallographic statistics) is referenced but not shown in the provided text; completeness and R-factors should be verified for the lower-resolution structures (3.2 Å RanBP10-Twa1, 3.5 Å R10_GLFF-Maea).
- The claim that the Twa1_52 interaction evolved toward "more entropically favored" binding across successive mutants is interesting but is based on calculated ΔS values from a limited number of variants; this interpretation should be presented more cautiously.

**Recommendation**
Major Revisions
The structural work is technically strong and the bidirectional specificity-swap experiments are elegant, but the absence of any full-length complex reconstitution or functional validation prevents the paper from fully supporting its central claims about reprogramming CTLH ring assembly; these experiments are essential for the work to meet eLife's standard for a significant conceptual advance.
