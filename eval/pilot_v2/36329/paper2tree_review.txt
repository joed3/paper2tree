**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** approach to dissecting feedforward and feedback processing in the ventral visual pathway using ultra-rapid serial visual presentation combined with MEG and MEG-fMRI fusion. However, the evidence supporting the central claims about recurrent processing is **incomplete**: the strongest claims concern the temporal dynamics of the initial processing sweep, while the critical feedback-related claims rest on statistically marginal effects and inferential leaps that are not adequately justified.

**Summary**

This paper uses ultra-rapid serial visual presentation (ultra-RSVP) at 17 and 34 ms per picture to suppress sustained neural activity, aiming to temporally segregate feedforward from feedback processing during visual object recognition. Using MEG multivariate pattern classification in 17 participants, the authors identify two processing stages: an initial bottom-up sweep that peaks progressively earlier with faster presentation rates, and the emergence of categorical (face vs. object) information that peaks progressively later. MEG-fMRI representational similarity fusion is then used to localize these stages, revealing bimodal dynamics in early visual cortex (EVC) where a second peak coincides temporally with categorical information emergence in inferior temporal cortex (IT). The authors interpret these findings as evidence for three "fundamental processing principles": that the brain compensates for disrupted feedforward sweeps through extended recurrent processing, that recognition delays index recurrent activity, and that feedback to EVC occurs simultaneously with categorical representation in IT. The paper positions itself as going beyond prior work by demonstrating feedback with natural images and localizing recurrent signals within the ventral pathway.

**Strengths**

- **Robust behavioral performance under ultra-RSVP** (d' = 1.95 ± 0.11 at 17 ms/pic, d' = 3.58 ± 0.21 at 34 ms/pic, P ≪ 0.001 for both above-chance performance and between-condition difference): This establishes that participants genuinely processed face identity under the extreme temporal constraints, validating that the neural signals studied are meaningful. The result is well-replicated in prior literature (Potter et al., 2014; Broers et al., 2018).

- **Progressive earlier peaking of the initial decoding sweep** (121 → 100 → 96 ms across conditions, with non-overlapping 95% CIs between 500 ms/pic and RSVP conditions, and replication in within-category analyses for both faces and objects): This is a well-supported empirical finding with appropriate statistical testing (bootstrap CIs, FDR-corrected permutation tests). The convergent replication across face and object within-category analyses strengthens confidence in the result.

- **Progressive later peaking of categorical information** (136 → 169 → 197 ms, with non-overlapping 95% CIs across all three conditions and corroboration by supplementary face-vs-object SVM classification): The inverse relationship with the early sweep peaks is a compelling and novel empirical observation. The categorical division measure is well-constructed and the statistical evidence is strong.

- **Decreasing decoding accuracy with faster presentation rates**, demonstrating less stimulus information is encoded under more challenging viewing conditions — a straightforward and well-supported finding that validates the experimental manipulation.

- **Creative experimental design**: The use of ultra-RSVP to suppress sustained activity and potentially segregate processing stages is genuinely innovative. The combination of MEG temporal resolution with fMRI spatial resolution through representational similarity fusion is methodologically sophisticated and goes beyond standard unimodal approaches.

- **Transparent reporting of limitations**: The authors appropriately acknowledge that the study does not inform on the precise nature of computations needed for evidence accumulation and that the necessity of recurrent activity for extracting conceptual meaning remains an open question.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **The central inferential leap — that delayed categorical information emergence implies recurrent processing — is not adequately justified and alternative feedforward accounts are not ruled out.** The paper assumes feedforward processing speed is invariant to input quality, but a purely feedforward cascade operating on degraded inputs (due to shorter exposure and forward/backward masking) could plausibly produce delayed categorical representations. Feedforward models with weaker inputs may require more evidence accumulation to reach categorical thresholds (cf. DiCarlo et al., 2012). The inverse relationship between early and late peaks, while striking, could arise from independent effects of masking: truncation of early signals (producing earlier peaks) and slower accumulation of weaker categorical evidence (producing later peaks). The authors should explicitly model or simulate feedforward-only alternatives to demonstrate that the observed latency shifts cannot be explained without recurrence, or substantially temper the claim. This weakness propagates to claims c1.4, c1.6, and the root thesis.

- **The key spatiotemporal evidence for feedback — the bimodal EVC dynamics — is statistically marginal and overclaimed (c1.5).** The second EVC peak at 202 ms for the 17 ms/pic condition did not reach significance, meaning the bimodal pattern is reliably demonstrated in only one of two RSVP conditions. Furthermore, for the 34 ms/pic condition (the one with a significant second EVC peak), the temporal coincidence between the second EVC peak and the IT peak yields p = 0.06, failing to reach conventional significance (c1.5.4). Paradoxically, the condition where the second EVC peak was not significant (17 ms/pic) shows significant temporal coincidence (p ≪ 0.001) — comparing a reliable peak to an unreliable one. This pattern of results does not constitute convincing evidence for IT-to-EVC feedback. The authors should either provide additional statistical support (e.g., Bayesian analyses quantifying evidence for the null vs. alternative) or revise the claims to accurately reflect what the data show.

- **The cross-paradigm, cross-cohort MEG-fMRI fusion design introduces untested assumptions that undermine the spatial claims (c1.3.5, c1.5).** The fMRI data were collected from a different participant cohort (n = 15) under 500 ms isolated presentation conditions — not during RSVP. The entire fusion analysis assumes that fMRI spatial patterns are preserved across fundamentally different viewing conditions, yet the paper's own thesis is that RSVP fundamentally alters processing modes. This creates an internal tension: the paper argues processing differs between conditions while assuming spatial representations are invariant. The authors should explicitly address this contradiction, ideally by providing evidence that fMRI RDMs are preserved across viewing conditions, or by acknowledging this as a significant caveat.

- **No formal quantitative metric is provided for the narrowing of temporal generalization patterns (c1.2.1).** The claim that RSVP suppresses sustained neural activity — a foundational premise of the paper — relies on visual inspection of Figure 4 rather than formal statistical comparison of off-diagonal breadth between conditions. A quantitative analysis (e.g., comparing the temporal extent of significant decoding at each training time point, or computing a width metric for the significant cluster) is essential to establish this central premise.

- **The claim that feedback signals originate "from within the ventral pathway" rather than from frontoparietal regions is not established (c1.7.3).** The study did not analyze frontoparietal regions, so feedback from attentional systems (Bar et al., 2006; Hopf et al., 2006) cannot be excluded. The timing argument (effects at ~170–200 ms precede typical attentional modulations) is indirect and not definitive, as early frontoparietal signals have been reported in some rapid processing contexts. This claim should be substantially moderated or supported by inclusion of frontoparietal ROIs in the MEG-fMRI fusion analysis.

- **The 100 ms boundary demarcating feedforward from feedback processing is assumed rather than established (c1.3).** The literature is not settled on this boundary — some studies report feedback effects before 100 ms (e.g., Hupé et al., 1998). The overlapping confidence intervals for the 34 ms/pic (94–107 ms) and 17 ms/pic (93–99 ms) peak latencies further weaken the claim that these conditions produce qualitatively different isolation of the feedforward sweep. The authors should discuss the sensitivity of their conclusions to this temporal boundary assumption.

- **The second EVC peak could reflect sensory processing of mask stimuli rather than feedback from IT.** Since mask images continue to be presented after the target in the RSVP stream, later EVC activity could represent mask-driven feedforward responses that coincidentally match the EVC fMRI RDM. This alternative is not discussed or controlled for, yet it represents a parsimonious non-feedback explanation for the bimodal EVC pattern.

- **Causal language ("compensates," "facilitates," "fills in missing visual information") is used throughout (c1.6, c1.5.5) but the study design is correlational.** No within-subject or trial-by-trial analysis relates early sweep disruption to subsequent categorical timing, and no causal manipulation (e.g., TMS to feedback pathways) tests whether recurrence is necessary. The 500 ms/pic data from a different cohort further weaken between-condition inferences. The framing of "fundamental processing principles" should be revised to reflect the correlational nature of the evidence.

**Minor Comments**

- The exact p-values for behavioral results are reported only as "P ≪ 0.001"; reporting precise values would improve transparency.
- MDS visualizations (c1.4.4) lack quantitative metrics (e.g., stress values, silhouette scores for categorical clustering) — adding these would strengthen what is currently a qualitative assessment.
- The extremely wide confidence interval for the 500 ms/pic onset latency (9–53 ms) suggests this estimate is unreliable; the 28 ms onset is physiologically very early for visual processing and may warrant discussion.
- The study uses only 12 face and 12 object exemplars from two categories (faces being a highly specialized stimulus class with dedicated cortical processing). The generalizability of findings to broader object recognition should be explicitly discussed as a limitation.
- Fixed button mapping for face present/absent responses is acknowledged but could have been avoided with counterbalancing across participants.
- The IT ROI definition (fusiform + inferior temporal cortex) is quite broad; specifying the functional or anatomical criteria more precisely would aid interpretation.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper presents a creative experimental approach and some well-supported empirical findings (behavioral performance, early sweep dynamics, categorical timing shifts), but the central thesis about recurrent processing rests on statistically marginal evidence (6 low-support claims including the critical MEG-fMRI fusion results) and inferential leaps that do not adequately exclude feedforward alternatives. The proportion of high-support claims (9/45) to low-support claims (6/45), combined with the fact that the most consequential weakly-supported claims bear directly on the paper's central thesis, means that the core contribution — distinguishing feedforward from feedback processing — is not yet convincingly established by the current evidence.
