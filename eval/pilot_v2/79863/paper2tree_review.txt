**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** contribution to understanding how postsynaptic retinoic acid signaling drives presynaptic homeostatic compensation through retrograde BDNF-TrkB signaling. The evidence is **solid** for the postsynaptic RARα requirement and the RA-induced presynaptic TrkB-dependent pathway under acute RA treatment, but **incomplete** for key aspects of the proposed mechanism under physiologically relevant CNQX-induced homeostatic conditions, owing to critical statistical concerns with omnibus tests and the absence of direct evidence for dendritic BDNF release.

**Summary**

This study investigates how postsynaptic retinoic acid (RA) and its receptor RARα regulate presynaptic neurotransmitter release during homeostatic synaptic plasticity. Using organotypic hippocampal slice cultures and conditional knockout mice with region-specific AAV-Cre injections to achieve pre- versus postsynaptic-specific gene deletion, the authors propose a retrograde signaling pathway in which: (1) postsynaptic RARα binds dendritically localized *Bdnf* mRNA splice isoforms (exon 2 and exon 6) and represses their translation; (2) RA relieves this translational repression, increasing local BDNF synthesis; and (3) newly synthesized BDNF acts retrogradely on presynaptic TrkB receptors to enhance presynaptic release probability. Additional correlative evidence links the developmental upregulation of BDNF expression to the maturation-dependent emergence of presynaptic homeostatic modulation.

The experimental strategy is creative and the overall logic of the pathway is coherent. However, several critical inferential gaps — most notably serious statistical issues with the CNQX induction data for BDNF and TrkB conditional knockouts, the lack of direct evidence for BDNF secretion from dendrites, and entirely speculative disease-relevance claims — prevent the central thesis from being as firmly established as the authors claim.

**Strengths**

- **Postsynaptic RARα requirement (c1.2):** The region-specific AAV-Cre deletion of RARα in CA3 versus CA1 of conditional knockout mice is an elegant and well-executed experimental design. High infection efficiency (>90%) is verified, and the results are replicated with both exogenous RA and CNQX treatments, with significant genotype effects in two-way ANOVAs for both mEPSC amplitude and frequency. This claim has strong evidence and is well-calibrated.

- **TrkB KO with RA treatment (c1.5 evidence):** The presynaptic TrkB KO experiment under RA conditions yields the most robust statistical support in the paper (F(2,147) = 14.87, p < 0.0001 for frequency), and the complementary pattern with postsynaptic TrkB KO not affecting frequency provides compelling convergent evidence for retrograde BDNF-TrkB signaling under acute RA conditions.

- **RNA-binding specificity controls (c1.3):** The in vitro RNA-binding assay includes multiple well-chosen controls: GST alone, RARα LBD ΔF, RARβ LBD, positive control (Gria1), and several negative controls (Psd95, Camk2a, Ef1a, Bdnf exon 1). These collectively provide reasonable evidence for selective RARα binding to Bdnf exon 2 and exon 6 transcripts.

- **Dissociation of pre- and postsynaptic homeostatic mechanisms (c1.5.4):** The consistent finding that BDNF and TrkB deletions affect mEPSC frequency but not amplitude across all conditions and genotypes provides a clean functional dissociation, supporting the model that BDNF-TrkB signaling is selectively involved in presynaptic modulation.

- **Developmental BDNF expression (c1.6):** Both in vivo hippocampal tissue and organotypic slice cultures show concordant developmental increases in proBDNF expression, with appropriate statistical analyses (one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's test), validating the culture system and providing a plausible temporal framework.

- **Synaptoneurosome validation (c1.4 evidence):** The preparation is verified with standard markers (PSD95 enrichment, histone H3 absence) and functional controls (RA-induced GluA1 increase), establishing confidence in the local translation readout.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Critical statistical invalidity for TrkB CNQX data (c1.5):** The omnibus F-statistic for the genotype effect on frequency under CNQX conditions is F(2,168) = 0.17, p > 0.8 — indicating essentially no evidence that CNQX effects on frequency differ across TrkB genotypes. Yet the paper appears to report significant pairwise comparisons (Figure 3F). Conducting post-hoc pairwise tests after a clearly non-significant omnibus test is statistically invalid and risks Type I errors. This directly undermines the claim that presynaptic TrkB is required for CNQX-induced homeostatic frequency changes, which is arguably the most physiologically relevant condition. The authors must either provide a statistical justification for this approach, report corrected analyses, or temper their conclusions to acknowledge that the TrkB requirement is demonstrated only under exogenous RA treatment.

- **Non-significant omnibus test for BDNF CNQX data (c1.5):** Similarly, the interaction term for BDNF KO with CNQX treatment on frequency is F(2,195) = 2.53, p > 0.05, yet significant pairwise comparisons appear in Figure 3C. While less extreme than the TrkB case, this weakens the claim that postsynaptic BDNF is required for CNQX-induced presynaptic homeostatic changes. The discrepancy between RA and CNQX results for both BDNF and TrkB KO experiments raises the possibility that CNQX-induced homeostatic plasticity engages additional signaling pathways beyond the RA→BDNF→TrkB axis described here.

- **Absence of direct evidence for BDNF secretion (root claim):** The retrograde signaling model requires that BDNF is synthesized postsynaptically and then released to act on presynaptic TrkB. While the genetic deletion data is consistent with this model, the actual secretion step — the defining event for retrograde signaling — is never directly measured. No BDNF secretion assay, BDNF-pHluorin imaging, or conditioned media experiment is presented. This is a significant gap for a study whose central thesis hinges on retrograde BDNF signaling.

- **Semi-quantitative RNA-binding assay without in vivo validation (c1.3):** The RNA-binding data relies on gel-based RT-PCR with purified recombinant proteins and total RNA, which is semi-quantitative and cannot assess binding affinity. No in vivo binding data (e.g., CLIP-seq, RIP-qPCR from neuronal lysates) is provided to confirm that RARα binds Bdnf mRNAs in intact dendrites, where competing RNA-binding proteins and RNP complexes would modulate the interaction. At minimum, quantitative binding data (qPCR-based enrichment) and/or demonstration that endogenous RARα co-immunoprecipitates with Bdnf mRNAs from neuronal extracts would substantially strengthen this claim.

- **Only proBDNF measured; mature BDNF not assessed (c1.4):** The synaptoneurosome translation assay measures proBDNF, but mature BDNF is the primary TrkB agonist. Without evidence that proBDNF is processed to mature BDNF and that this mature form is present in sufficient quantities to activate presynaptic TrkB, a key link in the proposed pathway remains unverified. Additionally, the proBDNF increase is only marginally significant (p < 0.05) without reported effect sizes or confidence intervals, and there is an inconsistency between the text (30 min treatment) and methods (10 min treatment) that should be resolved.

- **Speculative disease implications (c1.7):** The claim that convergence of BDNF and RA signaling has implications for neuropsychiatric disorders, particularly fragile X syndrome, is entirely speculative. No data in this paper examines BDNF signaling in any disease model, and the cited FXS studies did not examine BDNF-dependent presynaptic changes. This section should be clearly labeled as speculation or removed.

- **Developmental correlation not causally tested (c1.6):** The temporal correlation between BDNF expression increase and emergence of presynaptic homeostatic modulation is presented as supporting the model, but no experiment directly manipulates developmental BDNF levels (e.g., early overexpression or knockdown) to test whether this correlation reflects causality. The claim should be tempered to acknowledge its correlative nature.

**Minor Comments**

- The use of Mann-Whitney test as a post-hoc following two-way ANOVA is unconventional. While conservative for non-normal data, the authors should justify this choice or use standard post-hoc tests (e.g., Tukey's, Sidak's) with appropriate corrections.

- Throughout the figure legends, it is unclear whether the reported F-statistics represent genotype main effects or the critical genotype × treatment interaction terms. The authors should explicitly report both terms for all two-way ANOVAs.

- The claim of cross-species conservation of presynaptic homeostatic mechanisms (c1.1) overreaches the data, which comes only from mouse hippocampal slices. The Drosophila NMJ mechanisms likely differ at the molecular level and should not be equated with mammalian central synapse mechanisms.

- The argument that new synapse formation is unlikely to underlie the frequency increase (c1.1.2.3) is based on plausibility rather than data. Direct synapse counting (e.g., synaptic marker immunohistochemistry) would strengthen this point.

- The mIPSC data (c1.2.3) is presented only in supplementary figures and not pursued mechanistically. The authors should briefly discuss whether BDNF-TrkB signaling might also mediate the inhibitory frequency changes or whether a distinct retrograde mechanism is involved.

- Dendritic localization of Bdnf exon 2 and exon 6 transcripts is cited from Baj et al. (2011) in dissociated rat neurons but not verified in the mouse organotypic slices used here. Given that this is a key premise, confirming dendritic localization in the authors' preparation (e.g., by FISH) would be valuable.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The study addresses an important question with a creative experimental design, and several key claims — particularly the postsynaptic RARα requirement and the TrkB-dependent presynaptic effect under RA conditions — are well-supported. However, the critical statistical problems with the CNQX data for both BDNF and TrkB conditional knockouts (non-significant omnibus tests with reported significant pairwise comparisons), the absence of direct BDNF secretion evidence, and the lack of in vivo RNA-binding validation represent substantial gaps that must be addressed before the central thesis can be considered established to eLife's standards.
