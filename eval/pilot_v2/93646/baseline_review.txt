**eLife Assessment**
This valuable study provides solid evidence that CaBP1 and CaBP2 act synergistically to suppress CaV1.3 channel inactivation at inner hair cell ribbon synapses, though several mechanistic claims — particularly regarding the nature of the exocytosis defect, the interpretation of recovery kinetics, and the specificity of the gene therapy rescue — rest on incomplete evidence that would benefit from additional experiments and more rigorous quantitative analysis.

**Summary**
The authors investigate the combined roles of calcium-binding proteins CaBP1 and CaBP2 at inner hair cell (IHC) ribbon synapses by generating Cabp1/2 double-knockout (DKO) mice. Using patch-clamp electrophysiology, membrane capacitance measurements, auditory brainstem responses (ABRs), distortion product otoacoustic emissions (DPOAEs), single-unit recordings from spiral ganglion neurons (SGNs), and immunohistochemistry, they demonstrate that loss of both CaBPs results in severe hearing impairment that exceeds the phenotype of either single knockout. Enhanced CaV1.3 inactivation (both VDI and CDI) in DKO IHCs leads to diminished sustained Ca²⁺ influx and exocytosis, particularly at physiologically relevant holding potentials. Single-unit recordings reveal dramatically reduced spontaneous and evoked spike rates with strong adaptation. AAV-mediated re-expression of CaBP2 partially rescues Ca²⁺ currents, exocytosis, and hearing thresholds, supporting both the causal role of CaBP deficiency and the therapeutic potential of gene replacement.

**Strengths**
- **Comprehensive multi-level approach**: The study integrates molecular, cellular (patch-clamp, capacitance), systems-level (ABR, DPOAE), and single-neuron (in vivo SGN recordings) analyses, providing a thorough characterization of the DKO phenotype across scales.
- **Physiologically relevant recording conditions**: The authors go beyond standard patch-clamp protocols by testing exocytosis at depolarized holding potentials (−55 mV) and with sine wave stimuli that better approximate in vivo conditions, revealing a much more severe phenotype than traditional −85 mV recordings. This is an important methodological contribution.
- **Recovery from inactivation experiments**: The use of both brief (500 ms depolarization) and prolonged (2-min sine wave) conditioning stimuli to probe recovery kinetics adds significant insight into the mechanisms underlying the progressive nature of the in vivo phenotype.
- **In vivo single-unit recordings**: The demonstration that SGN firing recovers with longer interstimulus intervals (plateauing at 0.2 Hz) provides compelling in vivo evidence linking CaV1.3 inactivation kinetics to neural coding deficits.
- **Gene therapy rescue**: The partial rescue of hearing function by AAV-PHP.eB-mediated CaBP2 expression strengthens the causal argument and has translational relevance.
- **Clear demonstration of synergy**: The DKO phenotype is qualitatively and quantitatively distinct from either single KO, establishing functional redundancy between CaBP1 and CaBP2.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Inconsistent sample sizes and statistical reporting**: Throughout the figures, sample sizes (n and N) are often unclear or missing. For example, the number of cells and animals for train stimuli experiments (Figure 1D) are not stated, and the authors note that the subset used for train stimuli had atypically low inactivation (0.36 vs 0.47 average), raising concerns about selection bias. All figure panels should report n/N explicitly, and the authors should address whether the train-stimulus subset is representative.

- **Exocytosis efficiency claim requires stronger evidence**: The authors claim that reduced sustained exocytosis "cannot completely be explained by the diminished Ca²⁺ influx" (Figure 3C), suggesting an additional exocytosis defect. However, this conclusion is based on the ratio ΔCm/QCa, which assumes a linear relationship between Ca²⁺ charge and exocytosis. Given the highly nonlinear (power-law) dependence of exocytosis on Ca²⁺ at IHC synapses, this analysis is insufficient. The authors should either formally model the expected ΔCm given the observed QCa and the known Ca²⁺ cooperativity, or temper this claim substantially.

- **VDI/CDI dissection methodology**: The separation of VDI and CDI using Ba²⁺ substitution (Figure 2) relies on the assumption that Ba²⁺ currents faithfully report VDI without CDI. However, Ba²⁺ can itself influence channel gating, and the formulas used (from Tadross et al., 2010) have known limitations, particularly for channels with significant permeation-dependent gating differences. The authors should discuss these caveats more explicitly and consider whether differences in Ba²⁺ vs Ca²⁺ current amplitudes could confound the CDI/VDI estimates.

- **Interpretation of recovery kinetics is speculative**: The interpretation that faster recovery in DKO IHCs after 500-ms depolarization reflects faster Ca²⁺ clearance, while slower recovery after sine wave stimulation reflects "deep-state inactivation," involves two distinct mechanistic hypotheses invoked for the same preparation. Without direct Ca²⁺ imaging data, these remain speculative. The authors should more clearly delineate what is shown versus hypothesized and consider alternative explanations (e.g., different proportions of channels entering slow vs. fast inactivation states depending on stimulus paradigm).

- **Gene rescue controls and interpretation**: Several aspects of the rescue experiments need clarification: (1) Was a control virus (e.g., GFP-only) injected to rule out effects of surgery or viral transduction per se? (2) The construct uses CaBP2-L, but the authors note CaBP2-alt may be the predominant IHC isoform — could isoform mismatch explain the incomplete rescue? (3) The viral construct drives expression via a ubiquitous hCMV/HBA promoter, potentially leading to non-physiological expression levels and ectopic expression. The supranormal ABR wave I amplitudes in rescued animals are consistent with overexpression artifacts and warrant discussion.

- **BK channel analysis and causal claims**: The observation of reduced BK channel cluster size in DKO IHCs is interesting but the causal interpretation is uncertain. The authors speculate about CaBPs as transcription factors or indirect Ca²⁺ signaling effects but provide no mechanistic data. Given that BK channel expression changes are common in many cochlear mutants, this finding should be presented more cautiously.

- **Classification of in vivo single units**: The authors acknowledge difficulty classifying DKO units, estimating that ~13 of 16 are SGNs and 3 may be cochlear nucleus neurons. Pooling these populations is problematic given their different response properties. The authors should either provide stronger classification criteria or analyze the putative SGNs and CN neurons separately, or at minimum demonstrate that excluding the suspected CN neurons does not change the conclusions.

- **Comparison across studies with different conditions**: Figure 1C combines data from the present study with previously published data from different labs/conditions (different tonotopic positions, different ages). While acknowledged, this comparison is central to the paper's argument about synergy and should be validated with matched within-study comparisons where possible.

**Minor Comments**

- The abbreviation "t.i." (used twice, e.g., "visual (t.i. 'darkness') stimuli") appears to be a non-standard abbreviation. Please clarify or replace with standard English.
- Figure 1B shows peak-normalized traces but the un-normalized traces (Figure supplement 1B) suggest variable absolute amplitudes. Presenting both normalized and absolute traces side-by-side in the main figure would be more informative.
- The authors mention that the voltage shift in CaV1.3 activation is "somewhat surprising" given heterologous expression data but do not elaborate on potential mechanisms. A brief discussion of how altered subunit composition might explain this would strengthen the paper.
- Table 1 fit parameters should include confidence intervals or standard errors for the time constants.
- The discussion of clinical implications (hearing aids vs. noise cancellation) is interesting but speculative and could be shortened or moved to a separate perspective.
- Several supplementary panels are referenced but not easily linked to the main narrative — consider restructuring figure supplements for clarity.

**Recommendation**
Major Revisions
The study addresses an important question with a multi-level experimental approach, but the mechanistic interpretation of exocytosis efficiency defects, the recovery kinetics data, and the gene rescue experiments require additional controls, more rigorous quantitative analysis, or appropriately tempered claims before the conclusions are fully supported at eLife's standards.
