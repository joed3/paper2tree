**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a valuable identification of a post-translational modification signature—Ser13 phosphorylation and caspase-6-mediated cleavage of GFAP—associated with severe infantile Alexander disease, supported by solid evidence from patient tissue, iPSC-derived astrocytes, and reconstituted systems. However, several key claims rest on small sample sizes with acknowledged outliers, the central relationship between phosphorylation and aggregation contains an internal contradiction between in vitro and cell-based data, and the correlative nature of the PTM–severity association limits the overall strength of the conclusions to incomplete for the root mechanistic claim.

**Summary**

This paper investigates the hypothesis that disease-causing GFAP mutations in Alexander disease (AxD) converge on shared post-translational modification (PTM) changes that correlate with disease severity. Using phospho-proteomics on post-mortem brain tissue from 13 AxD patients harboring 10 different mutations, the authors identify Ser13 as the most abundantly phosphorylated residue on GFAP and show that pSer13-GFAP levels are selectively elevated in patients who died young. Phospho-mimic mutations (S13D/S13E) disrupted filament assembly and promoted aggregation in cells, and were associated with increased caspase-6-mediated cleavage at Asp225. In iPSC-derived astrocytes from an AxD patient (R239C mutation), pSer13-GFAP was found within perinuclear aggregate cores adjacent to nuclear invaginations. Caspase-6 protein and its cleavage product were selectively detected in young AxD patients but not in age-matched controls.

The study proposes that pSer13 phosphorylation and caspase-6 cleavage constitute a novel PTM signature linking diverse GFAP mutations to the most severe form of AxD. The authors commendably acknowledge that causality has not been established and that future interventional studies are needed. The work is significant for the AxD field and potentially for intermediate filament biology more broadly, though the mechanistic interpretation requires further substantiation.

**Strengths**

- **Multi-system convergence on pSer13 as a disease-relevant modification (root claim, c1.1):** The identification of pSer13-GFAP uses orthogonal approaches—LC-MS/MS phospho-proteomics and a validated phospho-specific antibody (KT13)—across patient brain tissue, iPSC-astrocytes, and transfected cells. The finding that this modification is elevated across 10 different GFAP mutations argues convincingly against mutation-specific artifacts.

- **Rigorous iPSC model with isogenic controls (c1.6):** The generation of CRISPR/Cas9-corrected isogenic controls, including a CRISPR control edited on the wild-type allele, with validation for pluripotency, karyotype, and off-target effects, represents a high-quality experimental platform. Multiple disease features are recapitulated, including elevated GFAP, high-molecular-mass oligomers, and perinuclear aggregation.

- **In vitro assembly studies with purified GFAP (c1.2.2, c1.2.3):** The demonstration that S13D completely abolishes filament assembly, forming globular non-aggregation-prone structures, while S13A produces short filaments, provides compelling biophysical evidence that Ser13 is a critical regulatory site for GFAP assembly. The graded response (S13A → WT → S13D) is internally consistent.

- **Caspase-6 as a disease-associated factor (c1.5, c1.5.1):** The striking binary pattern of caspase-6 expression—present in all 8 young AxD patients and absent in older patients—combined with age-matched non-AxD controls ruling out a developmental explanation (p<0.01), provides strong evidence for disease-specific caspase-6 upregulation.

- **Cleavage site validation and inhibitor studies (c1.4.2, c1.4.3, c1.4.5):** The D225E mutation reducing cleavage by >90% confirms the cleavage site, and the caspase-6 inhibitor data support caspase-6 involvement. The finding that cleavage inhibition does not resolve aggregation but causes filament bundling is an important and honestly reported observation that adds nuance to the model.

- **Scientific transparency (c1.7, c1.4.6):** The authors' explicit acknowledgment that causality is not established, that their overexpression system has limitations, and that outliers exist in both age groups enhances the paper's credibility substantially.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Internal contradiction in the aggregation claim (c1.2, c1.2.4):** The paper claims that Ser13 phosphorylation "promotes GFAP aggregation," yet the in vitro data show that S13D forms non-aggregation-prone globular structures, not aggregates. Aggregation occurs only in the cellular context (SW13vim- overexpression system), indicating that cellular factors—not phosphorylation per se—drive aggregation. This critical discrepancy must be reconciled. The authors should either modify the claim to state that phosphorylation prevents normal filament assembly (which, combined with ongoing protein synthesis in cells, leads to aggregation) or provide evidence for the specific cellular factors required. This distinction has direct implications for the proposed mechanistic model.

- **Small sample sizes with acknowledged outliers undermine statistical confidence (c1.1, c1.1.1):** The core phospho-proteomics finding rests on 8 young vs. 5 old AxD patients with one acknowledged outlier per group. With such small cohorts, marginal p-values (<0.05) are fragile and could change with outlier removal or addition of new samples. The authors should report exact p-values, consider non-parametric tests (Mann-Whitney U), and perform sensitivity analyses with outlier exclusion. The "young" group spanning 0.5–14 years is also problematically heterogeneous—a 14-year-old differs substantially from a 6-month-old in developmental terms. Subgroup analysis or treatment of age as a continuous variable would strengthen the analysis.

- **Single-patient iPSC model limits generalizability (c1.3, c1.6):** All iPSC experiments derive from a single patient with the R239C mutation, with one isogenic control clone and one CRISPR control clone. While the patient tissue data span 10 mutations, the cellular phenotypes (perinuclear aggregates, nuclear invaginations, pSer13 in aggregate cores) are demonstrated only for R239C. Additional patient lines with different mutations are needed to support the claim that these features are general to AxD rather than R239C-specific. At minimum, the authors should explicitly state this limitation and temper claims of generalizability.

- **pSer13-GFAP detected in isogenic control astrocytes complicates specificity claims (c1.3.2):** The finding that pSer13-GFAP is present in isogenic control iPSC-astrocytes (in filamentous form) directly undermines the claim from patient tissue that pSer13 is selectively associated with AxD. The discrepancy between in vivo (no pSer13 in controls) and in vitro (pSer13 in controls) is insufficiently addressed. The authors should investigate whether culture conditions (passage number, confluence, media composition, duration) influence Ser13 phosphorylation, and discuss the implications for the disease specificity of this modification.

- **Methodological inconsistency between AxD and control tissue preparations (c1.1.2, e1.1.1.2):** AxD patient GFAP was analyzed from high-salt-extracted (HSE) insoluble fractions, while non-AxD controls were analyzed as total lysates (Figure 2D vs. 2B). The HSE procedure may preferentially enrich for phosphorylated GFAP from aggregates, creating an apparent disease-specific enrichment. Control tissue should be processed identically (HSE extraction) for a valid comparison.

- **Caspase-6 inhibitor specificity (c1.4.2, c1.4.3):** Ac-VEID-CHO can inhibit caspases-3, -7, and -8 at the 10 µM concentration used. No complementary approaches (caspase-6 siRNA, CRISPR knockout, or dose-response studies) were employed. This is essential for the claim that caspase-6 specifically mediates D225 cleavage. At minimum, genetic knockdown of caspase-6 should be performed to confirm specificity.

- **Biomarker and therapeutic claims lack supporting data (c1.7.1, c1.7.2):** The suggestion that pSer13-GFAP could serve as a cerebrospinal fluid biomarker and that kinase inhibition could be therapeutic are presented without any supporting data. Given that pSer13 was detected in control iPSC-astrocytes and that causality is not established, these claims are speculative and should be substantially tempered or removed.

- **"New marker" claim for caspase-6 (c1.5):** The assertion that caspase-6 is a "new marker" for severe AxD requires demonstration that it has not been previously reported in this context. A systematic literature assessment is needed. Additionally, the term "marker" implies diagnostic utility, but no sensitivity/specificity analysis is provided.

**Minor Comments**

- The criteria for scoring cells as having "filaments," "aggregates," or "both" in SW13vim- cells are not defined, introducing potential subjectivity in quantification.
- Nuclear invaginations in iPSC-astrocytes are described qualitatively but not quantified (frequency, depth). EM images from isogenic control cells should be included for comparison.
- The EM-based claim that fibrous bundles correspond to GFAP aggregates (c1.3.3) would benefit from immuno-EM to confirm GFAP identity, as the bundles could represent other cytoskeletal elements.
- The 5% FDR cutoff for phospho-proteomic identifications is relatively permissive; the standard in the field is 1%. The authors should report how many of the 12 phospho-sites would survive a 1% FDR threshold.
- For the iPSC astrocyte differentiation, quantification of differentiation efficiency (percentage of GFAP-positive cells) would help rule out the possibility that differences in GFAP levels reflect variable differentiation rather than a disease-specific effect.
- Exact p-values should be reported throughout rather than threshold indicators (p<0.05, p<0.01).
- The DAPI-positive centers of perinuclear aggregates are compared to Rosenthal fibers, but no biochemical characterization (αB-crystallin, Hsp27, ubiquitin) was performed to validate RF identity. The term "RF-like" should be used cautiously.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper addresses an important question in a rare neurodegenerative disease and presents a creative multi-system approach, but the internal contradiction between in vitro and cell-based aggregation data, small sample sizes with fragile statistics, reliance on a single iPSC patient line, and lack of genetic validation of caspase-6 specificity represent substantive concerns that must be addressed before the central claims meet eLife's standard for convincing evidence. Thirty-eight of 56 claims have high support, indicating a strong experimental foundation, but the two low-support claims involve translational extrapolations that should be removed or heavily qualified, and several medium-support primary claims require additional experimental evidence or revised interpretation.
