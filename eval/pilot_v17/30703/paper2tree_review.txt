**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** investigation into how wound-induced reactive oxygen species regulate vimentin expression and collagen reorganization to promote caudal fin regeneration in zebrafish larvae. The evidence supporting individual steps of the proposed pathway ranges from **solid** to **incomplete**, with particularly strong genetic evidence for vimentin's role in regeneration but critical gaps in the NFκB-vimentin connection and in establishing a causal (rather than correlative) relationship between collagen fiber projections and epithelial cell guidance.

**Summary**

The authors propose a signaling pathway in which wound-induced ROS, generated by DUOX1, activate NFκB to drive vimentin expression in mesenchymal cells at the wound margin. Vimentin then regulates collagen gene expression (col1a1, col2a1b) and the reorganization of collagen fibers into projections that physically guide epithelial cell extensions at the wound edge, thereby promoting efficient caudal fin regeneration in zebrafish larvae. To test this model, the authors generated a vimentin promoter reporter line Tg(-2vim:egfp), employed morpholino knockdown, CRISPR F0 mosaic mutagenesis, and pharmacological inhibition (DPI, Withaferin A, BAPN), combined with second harmonic generation (SHG) imaging of fibrillar collagen, qRT-PCR, and a burn wound model. The study spans multiple levels of analysis and provides an integrative framework linking early redox signals to tissue-level mechanical processes during regeneration.

**Strengths**

- **Vimentin is required for fin regeneration (high support):** This is the strongest claim in the paper. Three independent loss-of-function approaches — two morpholinos (MO1, MO2), RNA rescue, and CRISPR F0 mosaic mutants — all converge on the same conclusion. Sample sizes are large (n=85–130 per group), statistical significance is robust (p<0.0001), and 95% confidence intervals are non-overlapping. The wound-specificity of the defect (no developmental fin phenotype) and the absence of apoptosis at the wound further strengthen this conclusion.

- **Wound-induced vimentin expression at the wound edge (high support):** Two independent methods — whole-mount in situ hybridization and live confocal imaging of the Tg(-2vim:egfp) reporter — demonstrate localized, wound-specific vimentin expression by 24 hpw, with appropriate controls (sense probe, temporal dynamics).

- **Vimentin reporter validation (high support):** The Tg(-2vim:egfp) reporter recapitulates expected vimentin expression patterns (cranial ganglion cells, spinal cord neurons, opercle, fin mesenchymal cells) consistent with Cerdà et al. (1998), and responds appropriately to oncogenic HRasV12-induced EMT.

- **Quantitative SHG analysis of collagen defects in vimentin morphants (high support):** Both wound edge contour length (p=0.0009) and CurveAlign fiber angle analysis (p=0.0003) demonstrate clear collagen organization defects in vimentin-depleted larvae, with good sample sizes and non-overlapping confidence intervals.

- **Wound-induced collagen expression depends on vimentin (high support):** qRT-PCR from wound-specific tissue samples demonstrates both wound-induced increases in col1a1 and col2a1b and their reduction upon vimentin depletion, with 5–6 biological replicates and appropriate statistical support.

- **Burn wound model provides convergent evidence (high support for descriptive observations):** SHG imaging clearly demonstrates loss of collagen fibers near the burn wound edge, and quantification shows significantly reduced projection formation (p=0.0116) and healing area (p<0.0001) compared to tail transection.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **The NFκB–vimentin connection is inadequately supported (low support):** The sole evidence for NFκB involvement comes from Withaferin A (WA), which the authors themselves acknowledge directly inhibits vimentin protein. This is a fundamental confound that renders the NFκB claim essentially untestable with this reagent alone. The paper needs either (1) a genetic approach to NFκB inhibition (e.g., dominant-negative IκBα, NFκB subunit morpholinos), or (2) at minimum, a second chemically distinct NFκB inhibitor without known vimentin activity. Without this, the claim that "ROS regulates vimentin through NFκB" should be substantially tempered or removed from the main conclusions. Notably, the WA effect on the EGFP reporter (a transcriptional readout) should theoretically be independent of WA's direct effect on vimentin protein, but this distinction is not discussed — raising questions about whether feedback from vimentin protein levels to promoter activity could explain the result.

- **The "physical guidance" claim is correlative, not demonstrated (medium support, critical interpretive gap):** The assertion that collagen projections "physically guide" or "push" epithelial cell extensions is a central claim of the paper, yet it rests entirely on temporal and spatial correlation from SHG/fluorescence imaging. No direct evidence of mechanical guidance is provided: no laser ablation of individual fibers, no integrin-blocking experiments, no force measurements, and no quantification of the spatial relationship between fibers and epithelial cells (e.g., distance measurements, contact frequency). The ~15-minute time resolution of SHG imaging may be insufficient to definitively establish temporal ordering. The authors should either provide direct evidence of guidance (perturbation of individual fibers) or reframe this claim as an association/correlation rather than a causal mechanical mechanism.

- **The burn wound model cannot distinguish collagen-specific effects from general tissue damage (medium support with critical confound):** Thermal injury causes extensive damage to cells, proteins, lipids, and multiple ECM components beyond collagen. The absence of projections and reduced healing after burn could reflect any of these factors. Without a rescue experiment (e.g., exogenous collagen application to burn wounds, or selective collagen disruption without thermal damage), the burn model cannot specifically support the collagen guidance hypothesis. The comparison between fundamentally different injury types (clean amputation vs. thermal burn) is inherently problematic.

- **BAPN experiments are compromised by toxicity (medium support):** BAPN caused lethality at 4–5 dpf, preventing the standard 3 dpw assessment and raising serious concerns about non-specific toxicity. The paradoxical increase in contour length (attributed to a measurement artifact) and the single-experiment-in-triplicate design further limit this evidence. A dose-response experiment and an alternative lysyl oxidase inhibitor or genetic approach would be necessary to strengthen this claim.

- **Projection quantification suffers from extreme variability (weakens multiple claims):** The binary scoring of epithelial projections shows extremely wide confidence intervals in control groups (e.g., DMSO CI: 31.76–99.58% for DPI experiments; amputation CI: 48.66–98.67% for burn experiments). This level of inter-replicate variability undermines confidence in the assay's reliability and raises questions about whether the observed differences reflect true biology or stochastic experimental variation. The authors should address this variability — ideally through blinded scoring, more replicates, and quantitative morphometric analysis of projections rather than binary scoring.

- **CRISPR F0 mosaic mutants lack protein-level validation:** The degree of vimentin protein loss in F0 mosaics is unknown. Only one guide RNA was used, and no quantification of mutagenesis efficiency per larva is provided. The smaller effect size in CRISPR mosaics compared to morphants (which could reflect mosaicism or MO off-target effects) should be discussed more critically. A Western blot or immunofluorescence for vimentin protein in mosaics is needed.

- **The identity of vimentin-positive cells remains unknown:** While several cell types are excluded (not krt4+ epithelial, not mpeg1+ macrophage, not pu.1-dependent leukocytes), the positive identity of the vim+ cells is never established. Without lineage tracing or single-cell transcriptomics, characterizing these cells as "mesenchymal" is presumptive. This is important because the mechanistic interpretation (vimentin in mesenchymal cells regulates collagen production) depends on knowing which cells express vimentin and produce collagen.

**Minor Comments**

- The DPI effect on vimentin reporter intensity (Figure 2J), while statistically significant, has a modest effect size (mean ratio difference ~0.12) with only 12 total fins and 2–6 fins per treatment per replicate. The sample size per replicate is very low and should be increased.

- The Duox1 MO result corroborating DPI is shown only qualitatively in supplementary figures without quantification — this should be quantified to match the DPI data presentation.

- The Larp6 morpholino experiment is described as "a single experiment performed in triplicate" for regeneration data — this should be replicated independently.

- The TUNEL assessment at only 1 hpw is insufficient to rule out cell survival effects. Later time points (12, 24 hpw) should be examined.

- The slight increase in developmental fin area in CRISPR F0 mosaics (Figure 3—figure supplement 3G) is unexplained and should be discussed.

- The translational claims in the Discussion (therapeutic relevance, collagen scaffold approaches) extend well beyond the evidence and should be more clearly flagged as speculative.

- The HRasV12 EMT validation of the reporter was performed in only two replicates without quantification — while not central to the paper, this should be noted as preliminary.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper addresses an interesting and significant question, and the evidence that vimentin is required for zebrafish fin regeneration is compelling. However, several claims central to the paper's narrative — particularly the NFκB–vimentin connection (supported by a single confounded inhibitor), the "physical guidance" of epithelial cells by collagen (entirely correlative), and the collagen-specificity of the burn wound phenotype — require either substantially stronger evidence or significant reframing of conclusions. Of 62 claims, 35 have high support, but 4 claims with low support include ones that are central to the proposed pathway, and multiple medium-support claims have critical unaddressed confounds that must be resolved.
