**eLife Assessment**
This valuable study presents solid evidence that Tet-mediated hydroxymethylation continues to remodel the epigenome during postmitotic Purkinje cell differentiation, but the central mechanistic claim—that active DNA demethylation via the TDG-BER pathway occurs in vivo in neurons—rests on incomplete evidence, as key intermediates (5fC/5caC) were undetectable and no genetic disruption of downstream pathway components was performed.

**Summary**
This paper investigates the roles of 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC) during postmitotic differentiation of cerebellar Purkinje cells (PCs). Using fluorescence-activated nuclear sorting to purify PCs at P0, P7, and adult stages, the authors generate matched datasets of transcription (RNASeq), DNA methylation and hydroxymethylation (OxBSSeq), chromatin accessibility (ATACSeq), and histone modifications (H3K4me3, H3K27me3 ChIPSeq). They describe the developmental progression of epigenetic marks, identifying a class of highly expressed, PC-specific genes that lose both 5mCG and 5hmCG over their gene bodies during terminal differentiation, forming unusually large DNA methylation valleys (DMVs). To test the requirement for continued Tet activity, they generate Pcp2-Cre-driven Tet1/2/3 triple knockout (Pcp2TetTKO) mice, in which recombination initiates at ~P7 in postmitotic PCs. They report that late-forming DMVs are blocked in the TKO, that hundreds of genes are transcriptionally misregulated, and that putative regulatory sites fail to undergo demethylation. The authors conclude that Tet-mediated active DNA demethylation—proceeding through further oxidation to 5fC/5caC and removal by TDG-BER—occurs in postmitotic neurons and is essential for proper transcriptional and epigenetic maturation.

**Strengths**
- The technical achievement of generating multi-omic, single-nucleotide-resolution datasets from FANS-purified Purkinje cells—one of the rarest neuronal populations in the cerebellum—across three developmental timepoints is exceptional. The use of OxBSSeq to distinguish 5mC from 5hmC is critically important given their opposing functional implications and is well justified.
- The experimental design of the Pcp2TetTKO is well conceived: Cre activation at ~P7, more than two weeks after PCs have completed their final division, provides a clean system to interrogate postmitotic Tet function without confounds from replicative dilution.
- The internal temporal control within the TKO experiment is elegant: DMVs established before P7 (e.g., Hox clusters, Calb1) are unaffected while those forming after P7 (e.g., Gpr63, Grid2) are blocked, strengthening the argument that ongoing Tet activity is required.
- The identification of distinct epigenetic signatures among developmentally repressed genes (highCHmod vs. lowCHmod) is a novel and interesting observation that adds nuance to models of transcriptional silencing in neurons.
- Data quality metrics are excellent, with high Pearson correlation coefficients across biological replicates and appropriate validation of bisulfite conversion and oxidation rates.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**
- **The central claim of "active demethylation" via TDG-BER is not directly demonstrated.** The authors acknowledge they cannot detect 5fC or 5caC, and they have not genetically disrupted TDG or other BER components in PCs. While Tet dependence is convincingly shown, the specific downstream pathway remains assumed. Alternative mechanisms—such as transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair or other DNA repair pathways—could also remove oxidized bases. The authors should temper their conclusion or provide additional evidence (e.g., TDG conditional knockout, or detection of BER intermediates such as AP sites at DMV loci).

- **Inconsistent statistical thresholds weaken the TKO transcriptional analysis.** Developmental differential expression uses stringent criteria (p < 0.01, log2FC > 2), while TKO analysis uses substantially more permissive thresholds (p < 0.05, log2FC > 0.5). This ~4-fold difference in fold-change cutoff likely inflates the number of TKO-affected genes (721 up, 548 down) and may exaggerate the apparent impact. The authors should either justify this discrepancy or reanalyze using consistent thresholds.

- **The functional consequences of the TKO are inadequately characterized.** Despite identifying misregulation of numerous ion channels and synaptic genes, no electrophysiological analysis was performed. The reported absence of motor deficits on the rotarod is surprising and seems at odds with the claimed importance of Tet-mediated epigenetic maturation for PC function. Either the transcriptional changes are functionally compensated, the rotarod is insufficiently sensitive, or the changes are less consequential than implied. More sensitive behavioral assays (e.g., Erasmus ladder, eyeblink conditioning) or electrophysiological recordings are needed.

- **The causal relationship between DNA demethylation and transcription is not established.** The paper demonstrates correlation between DMV formation and high expression, and shows both are Tet-dependent. However, it remains unclear whether demethylation drives expression or is a consequence of high transcriptional activity (e.g., through transcription-coupled Tet recruitment). The expression levels of DMV-associated genes in the TKO should be explicitly quantified and presented to address directionality.

- **Completeness and kinetics of Tet deletion are insufficiently characterized.** The paper shows PCR evidence of recombination and RNA evidence of exon deletion, but does not quantify residual Tet protein levels or enzymatic activity. Given that Pcp2-Cre onset is gradual and three loci must recombine, there is likely a prolonged transitional period with partial Tet activity. Western blots or quantitative proteomics for Tet proteins, and genome-wide quantification of 5hmC loss in TKO PCs (e.g., by mass spectrometry or dot blot), would substantially strengthen the interpretation.

- **Only two biological replicates are used for most assays.** While correlation coefficients are high, two replicates provide limited statistical power for identifying differentially modified regions, especially for the OxBSSeq data where estimation of 5hmC requires deconvolution from two noisy measurements. The authors should discuss the power limitations and consider whether key findings are robust to bootstrap or permutation analyses.

- **The specificity of DMV-associated genes to Purkinje cells is asserted but not rigorously tested.** Comparison with published OxBSSeq data from other neuronal types (e.g., granule cells, cortical neurons) would strengthen the claim that this is a PC-specific phenomenon rather than a general feature of highly expressed neuronal genes.

**Minor Comments**
- Figure 1F legend labels panels inconsistently (panel D appears twice, likely should be F).
- The paper would benefit from a summary figure/model illustrating the three proposed functions of 5hmC (passive demethylation in progenitors, functional demethylation in mature neurons, active demethylation in differentiating neurons).
- The discussion of MeCP2 binding and functional demethylation in the introduction, while relevant background, is somewhat lengthy relative to the data presented on this topic in the current study.
- The statement that mass spectrometry "failed to detect 5fC or 5caC" should include detection limits for the assay to allow readers to assess sensitivity.
- Several supplementary figures referenced in the text (e.g., Figure 3—figure supplement 1I-K) are not included in the provided manuscript; their availability should be confirmed.
- The Methods section does not describe the mass spectrometry protocol used for detecting modified bases in PC genomic DNA (Figure 3—figure supplement 1L).
- Gene ontology categories for TKO up- and down-regulated genes are described qualitatively; the actual enrichment scores and corrected p-values should be reported.

**Recommendation**
Major Revisions
The study addresses an important question and the multi-omic dataset is of high quality, but the central mechanistic claim of active demethylation via TDG-BER exceeds the supporting evidence, the functional significance of the TKO phenotype is insufficiently explored, and analytical inconsistencies need resolution before publication in eLife.
