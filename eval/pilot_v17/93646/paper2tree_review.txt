**eLife Assessment**

This study presents a **valuable** contribution to understanding how calcium-binding proteins CaBP1 and CaBP2 cooperatively regulate CaV1.3 channel inactivation at inner hair cell ribbon synapses, with evidence that is largely **convincing** at the biophysical and systems levels, though several mechanistic interpretations — particularly regarding direct CaBP roles in exocytosis and the viability of gene therapy — rest on **incomplete** evidence and require more cautious framing.

**Summary**

Oestreicher et al. investigate the combined roles of CaBP1 and CaBP2 in inner hair cell (IHC) synaptic function using Cabp1/2 double-knockout (DKO) mice. The central thesis is that CaBP1 and CaBP2 act in a partially overlapping manner to suppress both voltage-dependent (VDI) and calcium-dependent (CDI) inactivation of CaV1.3 channels, and that their combined loss — far more severe than either single knockout — leads to profound hearing impairment by depleting the available pool of activatable channels, collapsing sustained exocytosis, and degrading spiral ganglion neuron (SGN) sound encoding. The study integrates patch-clamp electrophysiology of IHCs (inactivation kinetics, exocytosis via capacitance measurements), in vivo single-unit SGN recordings, ABR/DPOAE audiometry, immunohistochemistry, and AAV-mediated CaBP2 gene therapy rescue. The authors propose that even mild tonic IHC depolarization drives CaV1.3 channels into a prolonged "deep-state" inactivation in the absence of CaBPs, effectively silencing synapses in vivo, and that viral CaBP2 delivery substantially — though incompletely — restores auditory function.

**Strengths**

- **Enhanced VDI and CDI in DKO IHCs (high support):** The Ba²⁺/Ca²⁺ substitution experiments using the Tadross et al. (2010) framework provide rigorous quantitative separation of VDI (~2-fold increase) and CDI (~6-fold increase) in DKO, with p<0.0001. The ~6-fold CDI enhancement is a particularly striking and novel finding, suggesting CaBP1 contributes substantially to CDI suppression — a role not apparent from either single knockout alone.

- **Preserved peak currents and channel clustering (high support):** The demonstration that maximal Ca²⁺ current amplitudes from short stimuli and CaV1.3 immunofluorescence clustering are comparable between DKO and WT is an important control establishing that the phenotype arises from altered channel gating rather than reduced channel expression.

- **Dissociation between RRP and sustained exocytosis (high support):** The finding that rapid exocytosis (RRP) is preserved while sustained exocytosis is severely impaired logically follows from the inactivation kinetics and is well-supported by graded capacitance measurements across multiple pulse durations with appropriate statistics.

- **Severe, progressive hearing impairment (high support):** ABR threshold elevation is unambiguous (59→68 dB SPL clicks across ages, N=7-9 per group), and the convergent multi-method assessment (ABR thresholds, wave amplitudes, latencies, single-unit SGN recordings) provides compelling evidence of drastically impaired sound encoding.

- **DKO far exceeds single KO phenotypes at every level (high support):** The systematic demonstration that the DKO phenotype exceeds either single KO across channel inactivation, exocytosis, SGN firing, and ABR thresholds provides strong evidence for partially overlapping CaBP functions. The novel exocytosis impairment present only in DKO (not in either single KO) is particularly compelling.

- **Activity-dependent worsening and deep-state inactivation (high support):** The convergent evidence from holding potential variation, sine wave stimulation, and in vivo stimulus-rate dependence of SGN responses (0.2 Hz plateau vs 150 ms in WT) coherently demonstrates that even mild stimulation drives DKO channels into prolonged inactivation. The double-exponential recovery kinetics in DKO versus single-exponential in WT is well-documented.

- **Substantial gene therapy rescue (high support for specific metrics):** ABR wave I amplitude recovery (p≤0.0001), restoration of sustained exocytosis and exocytosis efficiency, and partial threshold improvement upon AAV-CaBP2 delivery provide meaningful proof-of-concept evidence that the phenotype is largely attributable to reversible channel inactivation.

**Weaknesses / Essential Revisions**

- **Exocytosis efficiency interpretation is confounded by nonlinear Ca²⁺-exocytosis coupling (medium support for the claim that CaBPs have roles beyond channel modulation):** The reduced ΔCm/QCa ratio in DKO is interpreted as evidence for "additional impairment in exo- or endocytic processes," but this inference is problematic. Rapid CaV1.3 inactivation fundamentally alters the spatiotemporal Ca²⁺ profile at active zones; since the Ca²⁺-exocytosis relationship is highly nonlinear and depends on local Ca²⁺ concentration (not total charge), the efficiency ratio cannot straightforwardly distinguish altered Ca²⁺ dynamics from a direct exocytic defect. Additionally, remaining Ca²⁺ influx during sustained stimulation may be carried by non-synaptic channels, inflating QCa relative to synaptically relevant Ca²⁺. The authors should either provide direct evidence for CaBP roles in exocytosis (e.g., Ca²⁺ imaging at active zones, biochemical interactions) or substantially temper this claim.

- **The claim that CaBPs may act as transcription factors regulating BK expression is unsupported and biologically implausible (low support):** CaBPs are cytoplasmic calcium-binding proteins with no known nuclear localization signals or DNA-binding domains. The more parsimonious explanation — that BK reduction is secondary to altered Ca²⁺ signaling during development, as observed in CaV1.3 mutants (Brandt et al., 2003) — should be presented as the primary interpretation, and the transcription factor hypothesis should be removed or explicitly labeled as highly speculative.

- **"Largely preserved" cochlear amplification is an overstatement given DPOAE data (medium support):** Approximately 50% of DKO animals showed reduced DPOAEs, and at 7-13 weeks the threshold increase was statistically significant (p<0.002). Characterizing OHC function as "largely preserved" is misleading. The contribution of OHC dysfunction to elevated ABR thresholds in affected animals should be more carefully discussed, and the potential for CaBP expression in OHCs should be addressed.

- **Cross-study comparisons with single KOs introduce systematic confounds:** Key claims about the DKO exceeding single KO phenotypes rely on Cabp2-KO data from mid-cochlear positions (Picher, 2017) and Cabp1-KO data from Yang et al. (2018), acquired under different conditions, ages, and potentially by different laboratories. While the overall conclusion is likely correct given the magnitude of the differences, ideally the authors should acknowledge this limitation more explicitly and, where possible, provide context for how tonotopic position and recording conditions might affect the quantitative comparisons.

- **SGN recording data suffers from severe selection bias (acknowledged but inadequately addressed):** The 8-fold reduction in recording yield (0.3 vs 2.5 neurons/hour) means the detected DKO neurons represent the least affected fraction. This bias likely leads to substantial underestimation of the true SGN phenotype and complicates quantitative comparisons with WT. Additionally, the classification uncertainty (13/16 assumed SGNs, with 3 possibly cochlear nucleus neurons) should be addressed more rigorously, perhaps with post-hoc histological verification or additional physiological criteria.

- **Gene therapy "viability" claim is premature (medium support):** The rescue is explicitly incomplete (thresholds not fully recovered), uses the L-isoform rather than the potentially more abundant alt-isoform, lacks sham surgical controls, includes no long-term follow-up, and does not assess potential adverse effects of overexpression. The suprathreshold over-recovery (wave I amplitudes potentially exceeding WT) raises concerns about pathological overexpression. The authors should reframe this as a proof-of-concept demonstrating reversibility of the phenotype rather than evidence for clinical viability.

- **The paradoxical recovery kinetics after 500-ms depolarization undermines the claim that CaBPs speed channel recovery (medium support):** DKO channels recover fastest, directly contradicting the general claim. The post hoc Ca²⁺ clearance hypothesis is plausible but untested. The authors should acknowledge that their data cannot distinguish whether CaBPs speed recovery from inactivation versus prevent entry into deep inactivation states, and discuss how these two models lead to different predictions.

- **Attribution of the phenotype primarily to IHC dysfunction requires stronger support (medium support):** Caldendrin (CaBP1 splice variant) is expressed in SGNs (Yang et al., 2016), yet no direct assessment of SGN-intrinsic function is provided. The argument that CaBP1-KO has normal early hearing does not exclude delayed or cumulative effects of caldendrin loss on SGN excitability. The authors should discuss this limitation and consider whether electrical stimulation of SGNs (bypassing IHC input) could address this question.

**Minor Comments**

- The use of "indefatigable" in the abstract and conclusions is not supported by the data, as even WT IHCs show some inactivation. Consider replacing with "sustained" or "fatigue-resistant."

- Sample sizes for several key comparisons (VDI/CDI measurements, recovery experiments, injected DKO groups) are not consistently stated in the main text. These should be included for all primary claims.

- The assignment of 100 dB SPL for thresholds exceeding the 90 dB ceiling is arbitrary and should be explicitly justified or an alternative approach (e.g., censored data analysis) discussed.

- The C57Bl/6 background carries the Cdh23^ahl mutation causing age-related hearing loss. While unlikely to fully account for the DKO phenotype, its potential contribution to the progressive component should be briefly discussed.

- The unchanged adaptation time constant with greatly enhanced adaptation strength (12.5 vs 3.4) is an interesting dissociation that deserves more mechanistic discussion — it could reflect normal vesicle depletion kinetics with a reduced functional vesicle pool, which would be a distinct mechanism from channel inactivation.

- Statistical tests (Student's t-test) assume normality, which is not verified for most datasets. Non-parametric alternatives or normality tests should be reported.

**Recommendation**

Major Revisions

The paper addresses an important question with a well-designed multi-level experimental approach, and the majority of claims (43/61) are well-supported. However, the two low-support claims (direct CaBP roles in exocytosis and transcriptional regulation of BK) should be removed or substantially reframed, the exocytosis efficiency interpretation requires more rigorous analysis or tempering, the gene therapy viability claim needs to be downgraded to proof-of-concept, and the DPOAE data should be more honestly discussed. These revisions are essential to ensure the paper's conclusions are fully warranted by the evidence, consistent with eLife's standards for rigorous mechanistic interpretation.
